# Chapter One: The Iron Crag

## Scene 1 – The Fortress

The Iron Crag had been standing long before the Republic arrived.

The ancient volcanic crater rose from the blackened plains of Kalyph like the remains of some dead god. Its walls were jagged and uneven, their dark stone scarred by centuries of wind, rain, and war. Great sections of the crater had been carved away and replaced with durasteel, reinforced transparisteel, landing platforms, shield generators, and enormous blast doors.

But the Republic had never truly built the Iron Crag.

It had simply made the mountain larger.

From a distance, the fortress appeared almost impossible to comprehend. Towers of durasteel rose from the crater walls, connected by bridges wide enough to carry armored columns. Communications arrays stretched toward the sky, surrounded by rotating sensor dishes and defensive turbolaser emplacements. Gunships moved constantly between landing platforms, their engines echoing against the walls of the crater.

Thousands of lights burned throughout the fortress.

Some belonged to barracks.

Others illuminated hospitals, maintenance bays, command centers, armories, mess halls, vehicle depots, ammunition stores, communications stations, training grounds, and the countless corridors that connected them all.

The Iron Crag never slept.

It could not afford to.

For years, the fortress had been the center of General Rahm Kota’s campaign against the Separatists. Soldiers had arrived here wounded and returned to the front. Clones had marched through its gates as replacements and left as veterans. Mechanics had worked through entire nights repairing vehicles damaged in battle. Medics had learned to recognize the sound of incoming gunships before they ever saw them.

The fortress had grown with the war.

Every victory had added another hangar.

Every defeat had added another bunker.

Every battle had left another scar.

Deep within the mountain, entire battalions lived beneath layers of rock and durasteel.

The clone barracks occupied several enormous chambers carved directly into the crater’s interior. Long rows of identical bunks stretched beneath harsh white lighting, separated by equipment lockers, weapons racks, and narrow passageways. Thousands of white-armored soldiers could be housed within the fortress at any given time.

Beyond them were the militia camps.

They were different.

The volunteers of the 108th Republic Liberty Corps had built their own identity inside the fortress. Their quarters were less uniform, their equipment more varied, and their armor bore the marks of countless worlds and campaigns. Republic Army equipment stood beside locally manufactured weapons. Civilian transports were parked beside military vehicles. Some soldiers slept in proper barracks while others preferred canvas shelters erected inside enormous chambers.

The Iron Crag had room for all of them.

That was its greatest strength.

It was not merely a fortress.

It was a military city.

And the city had a harbor.

The northern face of the crater had been transformed into an enormous orbital docking complex.

Massive blast doors opened into the mountain, revealing hangars large enough to swallow entire squadrons of starfighters. Landing platforms extended from the crater walls, reinforced by enormous structural supports that disappeared into the rock below.

At the center of it all rested the fortress’s flagship.

A Venator-class Star Destroyer sat partially enclosed within the largest docking bay, its enormous hull extending beyond the mountain like the blade of some colossal weapon. Maintenance crews moved across its surface in swarms. Cranes lifted components into its hangars. Fuel lines connected its engines to the fortress below.

Beside it rested an Acclamator-class assault ship.

Two Arquitens-class command cruisers occupied smaller docking platforms farther along the crater wall, their crews constantly moving between ship and fortress.

And beneath them, secured within reinforced docking chambers, were two modified Pelta-class frigates.

Five capital ships.

All docked at the same fortress.

And the Iron Crag still had room for more.

The sound of the fortress was constant.

The deep roar of starship engines.

The clatter of armored boots against durasteel floors.

The hydraulic hiss of blast doors.

The distant thunder of artillery tests.

The shouted orders of officers.

The hum of generators buried beneath the mountain.

The voices of thousands of soldiers moving through the corridors.

Somewhere, a warning siren sounded.

Nobody stopped.

They had heard it before.

A squad of clone troopers marched beneath a massive Republic banner, their armor scratched and scorched from their latest engagement. A group of militia soldiers passed them going the opposite direction, carrying crates of ammunition toward a waiting transport.

Medics rushed a wounded clone through a corridor.

Mechanics argued over a damaged repulsorlift.

A communications officer ran past with a datapad clutched beneath his arm.

High above the main hangar, a lone figure occasionally passed behind the transparisteel windows of the command levels. General Rahm Kota was the only Jedi stationed within the Iron Crag.

Everywhere, the same thing.

Movement.

Work.

War.

The Iron Crag absorbed all of it.

It had become something more than stone and steel.

It was a fortress that remembered.

Every scar across its walls told a story.

Every blast door had been sealed during a battle.

Every hospital bed had held someone who had believed they would never see home again.

Every barracks had contained soldiers who had left through the gates and never returned.

And at the center of it all was the man who had made the Iron Crag his home.

General Rahm Kota.

To the soldiers stationed there, the fortress was more than a headquarters.

It was the place they returned to.

It was where they were healed.

Where they were resupplied.

Where they trained.

Where they mourned.

Where they prepared to go back to war.

The Iron Crag was their shield.

And if the Separatists ever came for it, they would discover exactly how difficult it was to break.

  

Deep within the fortress, far from the hangars and barracks, a set of enormous blast doors opened.

Beyond them was the command center.

The room was built into the heart of the mountain.

A massive holographic table dominated its center, projecting the Kalyph system in blue light. Dozens of smaller displays surrounded it, each showing troop movements, supply routes, communications traffic, planetary maps, or intelligence reports.

Officers stood around the table in conversation.

Clone troopers occupied several positions around the room.

Militia commanders waited near the far wall.

The air was thick with the quiet tension of men who had spent too long at war to ever truly relax.

At the head of the table stood General Rahm Kota.

The fortress continued moving around him.

But here, everything waited for his next order.

## Scene 2 – The Briefing

The tactical display shifted.

The enormous holographic projection of Kalyph disappeared, replaced by a detailed map of the planet’s eastern hemisphere.

Kota stood at the head of the table, arms folded across his chest.

Around him sat the men and women responsible for keeping the Iron Crag—and the war around it—alive.

Commander Vantage stood closest to the display.

The clone commander watched the map without speaking.

Beside him, Major Tally was already working through a datapad.

General Jarek Turnmaash leaned back in his chair, arms crossed.

Brigadier General Jaxen Kel-Zor sat beside him, his enormous cybernetic frame making the chair look almost too small.

Across the table, General Toren Trowk studied the map with quiet concentration.

Tala Vess’undri stood slightly apart from the others, her eyes moving between the tactical display and the intelligence reports in her hand.

Dr. Bor Regg sat near the end of the table, hands folded calmly in front of him.

Grissom Thruun’ka had somehow managed to bring a datapad nearly the size of a small serving tray.

Captain Drenn Craggor stood near the entrance.

He had not sat down.

He rarely did.

Kota looked at the display.

“Oros.”

The hologram zoomed toward the capital city.

“What’s changed?”

Tala answered first.

“The Separatists have increased their activity around the city.”

Kota looked at her.

“How?”

“Reconnaissance. Sabotage. Small arms shipments. Communications interference. Nothing large enough to justify a full-scale assault.”

Kel-Zor leaned forward.

“Yet.”

Tala glanced at him.

“Yet.”

The massive cyborg tapped one finger against the table.

“Then reinforce Oros.”

Trowk didn’t look up.

“No.”

Kel-Zor turned toward him.

“No?”

“We reinforce Oros, we tell them exactly what we think they’re doing.”

Kel-Zor gave a humorless smile.

“General, if they’re about to attack the capital, I don’t particularly care what they know we think.”

“They may not be attacking the capital.”

“Then why are they moving weapons into the surrounding districts?”

“Because they want us to believe they’re attacking the capital.”

Kel-Zor stared at him for a moment.

“You’ve been waiting all morning to say that, haven’t you?”

Trowk finally looked up.

“Since breakfast.”

Jarek chuckled.

“At least somebody’s having a good morning.”

Kota’s eyes remained on the map.

“Vantage.”

The clone commander stepped forward.

“I want more reconnaissance.”

Kel-Zor sighed.

“Of course you do.”

Vantage ignored him.

“We’re seeing too many gaps in our intelligence. Separatist units are moving through areas where our patrols aren’t operating. We don’t know their numbers, their commanders, or their objective.”

Tala nodded.

“That’s the problem.”

She touched her datapad.

Several red indicators appeared around Oros.

“They aren’t behaving like a conventional army.”

Kota looked at her.

“Explain.”

“The attacks are too small.”

She enlarged the display.

“Three attacks on supply convoys. Two communications stations sabotaged. Four attempted weapons transfers. One attack against a Republic patrol.”

Tally finally looked up from his datapad.

“That’s ten incidents.”

“Eleven.”

Tally frowned.

Tala pointed to another location.

“This one hasn’t been officially confirmed.”

Tally immediately began entering information.

“Then it isn’t eleven.”

Tala raised an eyebrow.

“Someone was shot.”

“That doesn’t make it an official engagement.”

“It makes it an engagement.”

“Not until the report is filed.”

Jarek looked between them.

“Does anyone else find it comforting that our intelligence officer and our logistics officer can argue about paperwork while the Separatists are shooting people?”

Grissom looked up from his datapad.

“Paperwork matters.”

Jarek smiled.

“Of course you’d say that.”

Grissom shrugged.

“Someone has to know where everything is.”

Kel-Zor pointed toward him.

“Tell him.”

Grissom looked at Kota.

“If General Kel-Zor wants to reinforce Oros, I need three additional freighters.”

Kel-Zor blinked.

“Three?”

“Four would be better.”

“I asked for reinforcements, not a fleet.”

“You asked for a reinforced brigade.”

“Yes.”

“Then I need the freighters.”

Kel-Zor leaned back.

“How much equipment do we have?”

Grissom stared at him.

“Enough for what we currently have.”

“And if we deploy?”

“Then we have less.”

Kel-Zor sighed.

“You’re impossible.”

Grissom smiled pleasantly.

“Thank you, General.”

Bor Regg quietly cleared his throat.

“If we’re discussing the possibility of a major engagement, I should point out that the hospital is nearly empty.”

Everyone turned toward him.

Bor continued calmly.

“We have enough beds, surgical equipment, bacta, and medical personnel to support a large engagement.”

Jarek frowned.

“That’s supposed to make me feel better?”

“No.”

Bor folded his hands.

“It’s supposed to make you aware that we’re prepared.”

Kota glanced toward him.

“How long can you maintain that?”

“Depends on how many people you send me.”

Kel-Zor gave a small grin.

“That’s the first thing you’ve said today that I like.”

Bor looked at him.

“I wasn’t complimenting you.”

The room went quiet for a moment.

Then Jarek laughed.

Even Vantage allowed the smallest smile.

Kota didn’t.

But his expression softened.

Only slightly.

He turned back toward the map.

“Craggor.”

The garrison commander stepped closer.

“The Iron Crag is secure.”

Kota waited.

Craggor knew what he meant.

“I recommend increasing readiness.”

Kel-Zor frowned.

“Against what?”

“Anything.”

Kel-Zor looked at him.

“That’s helpful.”

Craggor’s expression didn’t change.

“The Separatists know the Iron Crag is the center of our operations. If they’re probing Oros, they may be trying to draw our forces away from here.”

Trowk nodded.

“Which supports my theory.”

Craggor continued.

“If they’re planning an attack on the fortress, they’ll want as much of the garrison deployed as possible.”

Kota finally looked at him.

“What do you need?”

“Nothing dramatic.”

Craggor gestured toward the display.

“Increase patrols around the outer perimeter. Keep the defensive batteries manned. Lock down nonessential access points. Put the reserve companies on standby.”

Kota nodded once.

“Do it.”

“Yes, General.”

Vantage studied the map.

“There’s another possibility.”

Kota looked at him.

Vantage pointed toward a region south of Oros.

“They aren’t trying to take the city.”

Tala turned toward him.

“What are they trying to do?”

Vantage zoomed the display outward.

“Make us look there.”

The room fell silent.

Kel-Zor leaned forward.

“And where would you look?”

Vantage pointed toward the southern supply routes.

“Here.”

Tally immediately began checking the logistical data.

“Those routes supply nearly half the eastern theater.”

Grissom’s expression changed.

“That’s a problem.”

Jarek looked at him.

“How bad?”

Grissom tapped his datapad.

“If those routes are compromised, we lose food, ammunition, medical supplies, spare parts—”

“How long?”

Grissom looked at Kota.

“Three weeks before it becomes uncomfortable.”

Kel-Zor snorted.

“Uncomfortable?”

Grissom shrugged.

“After that, it becomes significantly more uncomfortable.”

Jarek laughed.

Trowk didn’t.

“Then we should move a brigade south.”

Kel-Zor immediately shook his head.

“Which brigade?”

“Mine.”

“You’d take the entire Second Expeditionary Brigade?”

“If necessary.”

Kel-Zor leaned across the table.

“That’s exactly what they’d want.”

Trowk looked at him.

“And sitting here is exactly what they’d want if I’m right.”

The two generals stared at one another.

Kota finally spoke.

“Enough.”

The room went silent.

Kota looked around the table.

“Tala.”

“Yes, General?”

“What do you believe?”

Tala paused.

“I believe the Separatists are testing us.”

“Testing what?”

“Our response time. Our communications. Our patrol patterns. Our command structure.”

She looked at the map.

“They’re trying to learn how we react before they commit to anything.”

Kota nodded.

“Vantage?”

“I agree.”

“Kel-Zor?”

The cyborg crossed his arms.

“I think we’re giving them too much credit.”

Kota raised an eyebrow.

Kel-Zor continued.

“They’re Separatists. They attack where we’re weak. We reinforce the weak point and break them.”

Trowk shook his head.

“That’s exactly how you get baited.”

Kel-Zor smiled.

“That’s why I have you around.”

Trowk sighed.

Jarek leaned forward.

“I think they’re watching Oros.”

Kota looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because Oros matters.”

Jarek gestured toward the map.

“It’s the capital. It’s where the politicians sit. Where the civilians live. Where the news crews go.”

He looked around the table.

“If I’m trying to make the Republic look weak, I don’t attack a supply depot.”

He pointed toward Oros.

“I attack here.”

Kota considered that.

Bor spoke quietly.

“And if they’re right?”

Everyone looked at him.

He gestured toward the city.

“Then there are millions of civilians between us and the battlefield.”

The room became quiet again.

Kota stared at the hologram.

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then Kota stepped closer to the display.

The capital.

The supply routes.

The surrounding cities.

The Iron Crag.

Everything was connected.

“Here’s what we know.”

He pointed toward Oros.

“They’re active.”

Then toward the southern routes.

“They’re moving.”

Then toward the fortress.

“And they’re watching us.”

Kota looked around the room.

“We don’t know where they’ll strike.”

His eyes settled on each officer in turn.

“Until we do, every road, every city, every depot, and every fortress is a target.”

Nobody interrupted.

“We increase readiness across the entire theater.”

Kota pointed toward Vantage.

“More reconnaissance.”

Vantage nodded.

“Yes, General.”

“Tally.”

“Already working on troop movements.”

“Jarek.”

“Second and third militia companies will be ready.”

“Kel-Zor.”

The cyborg straightened.

“First Brigade remains on standby.”

“Trowk.”

“I’ll prepare defensive contingencies for the southern routes.”

“Tala.”

“More intelligence.”

“Bor.”

“Medical teams will be ready.”

“Grissom.”

The quartermaster sighed.

“I’ll find your freighters.”

Kota looked at Craggor.

“Raise the Iron Crag’s readiness.”

Craggor nodded.

“Understood.”

Kota looked once more at the map.

“Nobody moves a brigade until we know why.”

He turned away from the table.

“Dismissed.”

Chairs moved.

Datapads activated.

Orders began flowing almost immediately.

The room came alive again.

Kota remained where he was.

For a moment, he stared at the holographic image of Oros.

Then he quietly said,

“Something’s coming.”

No one heard him.

And for now, Kota had no idea how close he was to being right.

## Scene 3 – The Tour of the Iron Crag

### Stop 1 – The Hospital

The command center doors closed behind Kota.

For the first time since the briefing began, the general was alone.

Not truly alone.

The Iron Crag never allowed that.

The corridors were filled with the sounds of boots against durasteel, distant machinery, and the muffled voices of soldiers carrying out the orders that had just been given. Technicians moved quickly between workstations. Clone troopers passed in small groups, their armor still marked by the remnants of recent battles.

Kota walked through them without slowing.

Several soldiers nodded as he passed.

He returned each gesture.

He knew most of their faces.

Some of their names.

Others he had learned to recognize by their armor markings, their voices, or the way they carried themselves.

He turned down a long corridor and approached a set of sliding doors.

A blue medical insignia was displayed above them.

The doors opened.

The smell of disinfectant met him first.

The hospital was quieter than the rest of the fortress.

Rows of medical beds stretched across the main ward, separated by curtains and small equipment stations. Medical droids moved silently between them. Nurses and orderlies walked from station to station, checking equipment and reviewing datapads.

There were empty beds.

A lot of them.

Kota stopped just inside the entrance.

Dr. Bor Regg was standing over one of the occupied beds.

The Mon Calamari physician looked up.

“General.”

Kota nodded.

“Doctor.”

Bor glanced toward the empty beds.

“You’re here to make sure we’re still useless.”

Kota frowned.

“What?”

Bor gestured around the room.

“Nearly empty.”

Kota looked at the ward.

“That’s good.”

“It is.”

Bor picked up a datapad.

“I’d prefer it that way.”

Kota walked farther into the room.

“How many?”

“Four.”

Kota stopped.

“Four?”

“Four.”

Bor gave him a tired smile.

“Don’t look so disappointed.”

“I’m not.”

“You have the expression.”

“I always have this expression.”

Bor laughed quietly.

“Fair enough.”

Kota approached the first bed.

A militia sergeant lay beneath a thin medical blanket, his left arm immobilized in a sling. A bandage wrapped around his shoulder and upper chest.

The sergeant noticed Kota.

He immediately tried to sit up.

“General.”

Kota raised a hand.

“Stay down.”

The sergeant hesitated.

Then obeyed.

Kota looked at the medical readout.

“Supply convoy?”

“Yes, sir.”

“What happened?”

The sergeant grimaced.

“Ambush.”

“How many?”

“Six attackers. Maybe eight.”

“Maybe?”

“It was dark.”

Kota nodded.

“Casualties?”

“None dead.”

Bor spoke from behind him.

“Three wounded. He’s the worst.”

The sergeant looked toward the doctor.

“I’ve been told I’m lucky.”

“You are.”

“Doesn’t feel like it.”

Bor smiled.

“That’s because you were shot.”

The sergeant gave a quiet laugh.

Kota looked back at him.

“How long until you’re back on duty?”

Bor answered before the sergeant could.

“He’s not.”

The sergeant looked offended.

“Doctor—”

“Two weeks minimum.”

“I can walk.”

“Wonderful.”

“I can shoot.”

“Even better.”

“I can carry equipment.”

Bor folded his arms.

“You can also bleed all over my floor.”

The sergeant looked at Kota.

“General?”

Kota shook his head.

“Listen to the doctor.”

The sergeant sighed.

“Yes, sir.”

Kota moved on.

The next two beds belonged to militia privates.

They had been wounded during a patrol, both suffering blaster injuries that had been treated quickly. One had a bandaged leg. The other had a burn across his side.

The first private noticed Kota approaching.

“General.”

Kota looked at his leg.

“How bad?”

“Doctor says I’ll walk.”

Bor nodded.

“He will.”

The private smiled.

“See?”

Kota looked at Bor.

“How long?”

“Three days before he’s walking without assistance.”

The private’s smile faded.

“Three days?”

“Unless you’d like to lose the leg.”

“No, sir.”

“Then three days.”

Kota moved to the other private.

“What’s your name?”

The young man looked surprised.

“Volsen, General.”

Kota nodded.

“Where were you hit?”

“South patrol route.”

“Same place as the convoy?”

“Different route.”

Kota’s expression tightened.

“How many attackers?”

“Four.”

“Vehicles?”

“No.”

“Uniforms?”

“No.”

Kota looked toward Bor.

The doctor said nothing.

Kota looked back at the private.

“Did you see their faces?”

“No, sir.”

“Anything unusual?”

The private thought for a moment.

“They knew where we were going.”

Kota’s eyes narrowed.

“How?”

“I don’t know.”

Kota nodded.

“Neither do I.”

He moved on.

The fourth bed was tucked slightly farther from the others.

A clone corporal lay beneath the medical lights.

His armor was stacked neatly beside the bed.

His helmet sat on top of it.

Kota stopped.

The clone was awake.

He looked toward the general.

“Sir.”

Kota looked at the medical readout.

“Corporal CT-—”

He stopped.

Then looked at the clone.

“What’s your name?”

The clone hesitated.

“CT-4472, sir.”

Kota shook his head.

“Your name.”

The clone stared at him for a moment.

“Vek, sir.”

Kota nodded.

“Vek.”

“Yes, sir.”

“What happened?”

“Patrol.”

Kota looked at the wound.

“Blaster?”

“Yes, sir.”

“How close?”

Vek thought about it.

“Close enough.”

Bor looked over.

“The shot passed through the armor and clipped the shoulder. He’s lucky.”

Kota looked at the clone.

“How do you feel?”

“Like I’ve been shot.”

Bor smiled.

“He’s been making jokes since he woke up.”

Vek looked at the doctor.

“Helps.”

Kota pulled a chair closer to the bed.

He sat.

Vek seemed surprised.

Kota didn’t seem to notice.

“How many were with you?”

“Four.”

“Everyone else?”

“Fine.”

Kota nodded.

“Good.”

For a moment, neither spoke.

Kota looked at Vek’s armor.

The white plates were scratched and stained from the patrol.

“You keep your armor clean.”

Vek glanced toward it.

“Commander Vantage says dirty armor makes dirty soldiers.”

Kota almost smiled.

“Vantage says a lot of things.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Most of them useful.”

Vek smiled.

Kota stood.

“Get some rest.”

“Yes, General.”

Kota took a few steps before stopping.

He looked back.

“Vek.”

The clone looked at him.

“You did well.”

The corporal’s expression changed.

For just a moment, the soldier looked less like one of thousands of identical white-armored clones and more like an individual who had been seen.

“Thank you, General.”

Kota nodded.

Then he walked away.

Bor followed him toward the exit.

For several seconds, neither spoke.

When they reached the doors, Bor finally said,

“You know, most generals don’t visit their wounded personally.”

Kota kept walking.

“Most generals don’t command my soldiers.”

Bor smiled faintly.

“That’s true.”

The doors opened.

The noise of the fortress returned.

Kota stopped just outside.

Bor stood beside him.

“General.”

Kota looked at him.

Bor’s expression had become more serious.

“We’re fine now.”

Kota waited.

“But if the fighting around Oros gets worse…”

He glanced back through the doors toward the hospital.

“We won’t stay this empty.”

Kota looked into the ward.

At the four wounded soldiers.

At the rows of empty beds.

At the medical droids waiting silently beside them.

He understood.

“I know.”

Bor nodded.

Kota turned toward the corridor.

“Keep them alive.”

Bor gave him a quiet smile.

“That’s what I do.”

Kota continued down the corridor.

Behind him, the hospital doors closed.

The Iron Crag kept moving.

### Stop 2 – The Vehicle Maintenance Hangar

The hospital doors closed behind Kota.

The quiet of the medical ward disappeared almost immediately.

The farther he walked through the Iron Crag, the louder the fortress became.

Boots struck durasteel.

Technicians shouted instructions across corridors.

Hydraulic systems hissed behind reinforced walls.

Somewhere beneath his feet, enormous machinery rumbled to life.

Kota followed the sound.

He turned a corner and entered one of the fortress’s largest internal chambers.

The vehicle maintenance hangar.

The chamber stretched farther than Kota could see from the entrance.

Several levels of maintenance platforms surrounded the central floor, connected by walkways, lifts, and suspended gantries. Republic vehicles occupied nearly every available bay.

AT-RTs stood on elevated platforms with their armor panels removed.

Speeder bikes waited in rows.

Armored transports were suspended above the floor while mechanics worked beneath them.

A pair of artillery pieces were being stripped down near the far wall.

Everywhere Kota looked, someone was working.

The hangar had the organized chaos of a battlefield that had somehow been brought indoors.

Mechanics moved between vehicles carrying tools and replacement parts.

Droids transported heavy components across the floor.

Crates of spare equipment were stacked against the walls.

A damaged speeder rolled past Kota with half its engine exposed.

The driver noticed him.

“General!”

Kota gave him a short nod.

The speeder continued on.

Kota walked farther into the hangar.

A familiar voice reached him through the noise.

“You’re holding it wrong.”

Another voice answered.

“I’ve been holding it this way for five minutes.”

“Exactly.”

Kota stopped.

He recognized the first voice.

Ash.

He followed it toward the center of the hangar.

A Republic infantry transport sat elevated above the maintenance floor.

Its rear section had been opened, exposing the drive system.

Three mechanics were working around it.

One was a veteran clone mechanic wearing a grease-stained work uniform.

The other two were Ash and Novek Volsign.

Ash had somehow managed to get grease across nearly every exposed part of his armor.

Novek was lying beneath the vehicle with a hydrospanner in one hand.

The clone mechanic was holding a datapad and looking increasingly irritated.

“Give me the stabilizer.”

Ash handed him a component.

The mechanic stared at it.

“That’s a coolant regulator.”

Ash looked down at his empty hand.

“Oh.”

Novek’s voice came from beneath the vehicle.

“I told you.”

Ash looked toward him.

“You said I was holding the wrong part.”

“That is what I said.”

“You could’ve told me which one was right.”

“You’ve been working on the same vehicle for twenty minutes.”

Ash shrugged.

“I was getting there.”

The mechanic closed his eyes for a moment.

“General.”

Kota stepped closer.

The mechanic straightened.

“General Kota.”

Kota looked at the vehicle.

“Problem?”

The mechanic glanced toward Ash and Novek.

“Yes.”

Ash immediately shook his head.

“No.”

Novek slid out from beneath the transport.

“Maybe.”

The mechanic pointed at him.

“That’s the problem.”

Kota looked between them.

“What happened?”

## CHP2 Scene 9 — Quiet After the Storm

Back when the Clone Wars were still new.

No confusion.

Almost lost beneath the distant sounds of the city.

Recognition.

The Network.

Missing in action.

# Chapter Three — The Last Day of the Republic

## CHP3 Scene 1 — Departure from Oros

Inside the command post, the battle was finally beginning to give way to recovery.

Tactical displays still covered the walls, showing the positions of Republic forces throughout Oros. Most of the markers had turned blue. The remaining red signals were scattered across the outer districts, where patrols were still sweeping through damaged streets and abandoned Separatist positions.

Kota stood before the central display with his arms folded.

The capital district was secure.

The main avenues had been cleared.

The surviving Kalyph Defense Force units were reorganizing, while medical teams continued working through the wounded. Fires still burned across the skyline, but the city was no longer under immediate threat of capture.

It had cost them heavily.

Kota studied the display in silence.

Behind him, officers moved between terminals, relaying reports and updating tactical maps. The room was quieter than it had been during the battle, but nobody had relaxed. Too many soldiers were still outside. Too many wounded were still being counted.

A militia communications officer approached from one of the side consoles.

“General?”

Kota turned.

“What?”

Kota looked toward the tactical display.

“How many?”

Kota nodded slowly.

That was what they needed.

The 108th had been fighting almost continuously since landing on Oros. The militia brigades were scattered across multiple fronts, and even the soldiers who had survived without serious wounds were running on exhaustion.

“Can they hold the city?” Kota asked.

“According to the reports, yes. The Senator’s reinforcements are already coordinating with the local command structure.”

“Of course you are.”

The command post returned to its usual background noise.

Kota turned toward the officers gathered around the central display.

“Listen up.”

Several officers stopped what they were doing and faced him.

Kota pointed toward the tactical map.

“Planetary Defense will assume security of Oros.”

Kota continued.

“All Republic combat elements, prepare to return to the Iron Crag for reorganization and replenishment.”

There was no ceremony in it.

No speech about victory.

They had secured the capital, broken the Separatist assault, and rescued the Senator. That was enough.

“Preparing transmission.”

108th Clone Regiment — RECEIVED.

Another pause.

“Reconnaissance elements acknowledging.”

Across Oros, blue markers began shifting.

Some units started withdrawing from defensive positions. Others began gathering at designated extraction points. Heavy vehicles were being prepared for transport, while clone troopers and militia soldiers received orders to return to their staging areas.

Not because the war was over.

Because, for the moment, Oros no longer needed them.

To all Republic combat elements operating on Oros: planetary defense forces will assume responsibility for city security. All Republic units are to disengage from current operations and prepare for return to the Iron Crag. Report to assigned extraction points for reorganization, medical evaluation, and replenishment. Then the confirmations continued.

One after another.

Across the city, Kota’s soldiers began leaving their positions.

The last day of the Republic was approaching.

But for now, Oros had survived.

  

## CHP3 Scene 2 — Xorad

Around him, machines hummed.

Holodisplays flickered.

Droids moved between consoles, processing reports from a battlefield that no longer existed in their favor.

Xorad stopped.

The primary holotable displayed the city.

Red indicators representing Separatist forces were disappearing one after another.

Company.

Battalion.

Division.

Gone.

His calculations had failed.

For three hundred and forty-two days, Xorad had studied General Rahm Kota.

He had mapped his movements.

His response times.

His defensive patterns.

His supply routes.

His habits.

He had studied every battle report available to the Separatist Alliance and constructed thousands of possible outcomes.

Kota’s army should have broken.

Instead, it had done the opposite.

The unexpected intervention of an unidentified Jedi had disrupted Xorad’s calculations.

Kota’s militia had refused to collapse under pressure.

And his two thousand elite clones had fought with a level of cohesion that Xorad’s models had repeatedly classified as statistically unsustainable.

The assault on Oros had failed.

Completely.

Xorad stared at the holotable.

Then his head turned toward the command console.

“Realign all remaining planetary divisions.”

A T-series tactical droid approached.

“General Xorad, ninety-seven point two-one-one-five-three-eight-five percent of our entire corps has been destroyed.”

“Only three full battalions remain operational.”

The tactical droid paused.

“A full commitment against the entrenched Republic position will result in the complete loss of our remaining forces. Victory is statistically impossible.”

Xorad finally looked at him.

“Then we shall alter the calculation.”

The tactical droid’s photoreceptors narrowed.

“General?”

“Prepare all remaining battle droids.”

“For what purpose?”

Xorad turned back toward the holotable.

“For an assault on the Iron Crag.”

“General, the Iron Crag is heavily fortified. Its defensive capabilities exceed those of Oros. A direct assault would result in—”

“I know.”

“Then I must advise against—”

“Advice noted.”

Xorad returned to the console.

His metallic fingers moved across the controls.

The fortress appeared above the table.

Massive.

Layered.

Defended from every direction.

His processors ran another simulation.

The result appeared in red.

PROBABILITY OF SUCCESS: 0.73%

Xorad stared at the number.

Less than one percent.

The tactical droid stepped closer.

“General, this confirms my assessment.”

Xorad said nothing.

The simulation continued running.

He watched the numbers change.

0.71%.

0.69%.

0.74%.

Then stabilize.

Xorad’s head tilted slightly.

“Kota has demonstrated an anomaly.”

The tactical droid waited.

“He repeatedly achieves victory when conventional probability predicts defeat.”

“General, that is precisely why the previous operation failed.”

“No.”

Xorad looked at him.

“That is why the previous operation succeeded.”

The tactical droid remained silent.

Xorad continued.

“My models assumed that Kota would behave rationally when confronted with overwhelming force.”

“He did not.”

The image changed.

Kota’s forces appeared across the battlefield.

“He relied upon loyalty.”

Another image.

Militia and clones fighting side by side.

“He relied upon individuals acting beyond their predicted limits.”

Another.

The unidentified Jedi.

“And he accepted variables that no algorithm could reliably measure.”

Xorad turned away from the holotable.

“Heart. Loyalty. Sacrifice. Defiance.”

His voice remained completely mechanical.

Yet something had changed.

“I classified these variables as weaknesses.”

He looked back toward the fortress.

“I was incorrect.”

The tactical droid processed this.

“Then what is your conclusion?”

“The illogical can overcome the inevitable.”

“Therefore, we will become illogical.”

“Exactly.”

Across the subterranean base, dormant battle droids began powering up.

Thousands of metallic feet struck the floors.

Weapon systems activated.

Transport vessels began preparing for launch.

The tactical droid looked toward Xorad.

“If we proceed, General, there is a high probability that none of us will survive.”

Xorad watched the fortress disappear from the holotable.

“Then we will ensure that our failure is worthy of the Separatist Alliance.”

For the first time since the Battle of Oros began, General Xorad had stopped calculating how to survive.

He was calculating how to die.

## CHP3 Scene 3 — Return to the Iron Crag

Alive.

People arguing.

People laughing.

Comrades.

“So get yourselves examined. If you’ve got one of these things, get it out. Don’t let whoever put it there decide what you do with your lives.”

He paused.

“We’ve spent our lives fighting for the Republic. Whatever happens next, we should at least have the right to decide who we fight for.”

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then a clone near the front stepped forward.

“He’s right.”

Another nodded.

“Get them out.”

A sergeant turned toward his squad.

“If I’ve got one of those things in my head, I want it gone.”

“Same here.”

“Put me down for examination.”

The voices began spreading through the formation.

Not everyone reacted the same way.

Some remained silent, still trying to process what they’d heard. A few officers exchanged concerned looks, while others openly questioned how something like this could have happened without anyone discovering it.

One clone near the back raised his voice.

“What if the surgery goes wrong?”

Bor stepped forward.

“We’ll explain the risks before each procedure. We have the equipment, we have the coordinates, and we have a successful extraction to guide us. We’ll do everything possible to keep you safe.”

The clone nodded, although his expression remained uneasy.

Another voice followed.

“How long is this going to take?”

“That depends on how many implants we find,” Bor answered. “We have three hundred and fifty surgical stations available. We’ll work in organized groups, and the medical teams will continue operating until everyone has been examined.”

Kota stepped forward again.

“Listen carefully. Nobody is being treated as a suspect here. Nobody is being punished. You’re not being accused of anything.”

He looked toward Vantage.

“What happened to him could happen to any of you. That’s why we’re doing this.”

Kota glanced toward the medical stations.

“Once you’re called, you’ll report to the medical personnel. They’ll conduct the examination and explain what needs to happen. If an implant is found, it’ll be removed.”

He paused.

“Anyone with questions can speak to the medical officers. Anyone with concerns can raise them through their commanders. But I want everyone cooperating. We cannot afford to waste time.”

A clone officer raised his hand.

“General, what about the rest of the personnel aboard the ships? The pilots, engineers, and support crews?”

“We’ll get to them,” Kota replied. “Nobody gets overlooked.”

He looked toward the assembled ranks.

“Your brothers who are still recovering from the battle will be examined as soon as their condition allows. The medical teams will prioritize those who can safely undergo the procedure.”

Kota let his eyes travel across the hangar.

“We’re going to take care of our own.”

That was enough.

The gathering began to settle. Officers turned toward their respective units, discussing examination schedules and making sure their personnel understood the instructions.

Some clones remained near the medical display, asking Bor further questions. Others began volunteering for the first available examinations.

The uncertainty hadn’t disappeared, but something had changed.

They understood what was at stake.

Vantage stepped back from the platform, looking exhausted.

Kota approached him.

“That was good.”

Vantage gave a tired nod.

“They needed to hear it.”

“They did.”

Vantage looked across the hangar.

“I still can’t believe it.”

“Neither can I.”

The two stood quietly for a moment.

Then Vantage turned toward the medical stations.

“How soon can we start?”

Kota glanced toward Bor.

“Doctor?”

Bor was already coordinating with the medical technicians.

“We’re ready to begin. The first stations are operational, and the remaining equipment is being brought online.”

Kota nodded.

“Then get started.”

Bor turned toward his medical team and began issuing instructions.

Across the hangar, medical droids activated their diagnostic systems. Personnel began moving into the designated examination areas, while militia volunteers helped organize the flow of soldiers and secure the surrounding facilities.

The first clones began stepping forward.

Vantage watched them go.

For the first time since the transmission, he wasn’t standing alone.

And neither were his brothers.

## Epilogue — The Senator

Kalyph — Capital City of Oros

The Empire had arrived.

Imperial transports descended through the atmosphere of Kalyph, carrying thousands of stormtroopers and military personnel. Their arrival had brought the capital of Oros to a standstill. Citizens watched from windows and streets as Imperial forces established their presence throughout the city.

Their objective was straightforward: find General Rahm Kota and determine what had happened to his forces.

Unfortunately for the Empire, Senator Corin Trotz had been less than cooperative.

At the top of the grand staircase leading to the capital building stood Trotz, accompanied by his personal detachment of Kalyph Defense Force soldiers and several members of the Habiran Guard. The latter were among the finest military personnel Kalyph had to offer, assigned exclusively to protect the senator.

An Imperial contingent approached the staircase and stopped several meters from the assembled Kalyph personnel. An Imperial officer stepped forward.

Trotz immediately recognized him.

“I’ve told you before. I don’t know what happened to General Kota, nor do I know where he went,” the senator began. “And I certainly don’t condone this unprovoked visit. Or should I say invasion?”

The officer maintained his composure.

“Please, Senator. We both know you know more than you’re telling us. But there’s no need to waste your breath on me any longer.”

He stepped aside.

“Someone else will be handling this situation.”

Behind the Imperial formation, a shuttle descended and settled onto the landing platform. Its engines powered down, and the ramp slowly lowered.

A figure emerged.

He wore black armor and a dark cape. His mechanical breathing was unmistakable as he began walking toward the capital building.

Trotz watched him approach, uncertain of what he was looking at.

The Imperial officer turned toward the senator.

“Lord Vader.”

Vader continued up the staircase, his attention fixed on Trotz.

“No more lies, Senator. You will tell me what happened to General Kota.”

The Habiran Guard immediately moved closer to Trotz, tightening their protective formation as Vader approached.

“I’ve already told your people that I don’t know—”

Trotz never finished his sentence.

Vader raised his hand, and the senator was suddenly lifted from the ground, his throat caught in an invisible grip.

“Senator!” one of the Habiran Guards shouted.

Without hesitation, both guards rushed forward, raising their specialized electrostaffs and striking at Vader.

Vader released his grip on the senator, allowing him to fall, and ignited his lightsaber.

The first guard struck.

Vader effortlessly intercepted the attack, turning his blade against the second guard as he attempted to flank him. Their weapons clashed against his lightsaber, but the Dark Lord barely moved.

One of the guards was suddenly thrown backward by an invisible force.

The other continued fighting.

“Get the senator inside!” a KDF soldier shouted.

Two soldiers rushed forward, grabbing Trotz and pulling him away from the confrontation. Another KDF trooper instinctively opened fire toward the Imperial formation, and within seconds, the staircase erupted into a firefight.

KDF soldiers exchanged blaster fire with Imperial stormtroopers as both sides scrambled for cover.

As Trotz was rushed through the entrance, he briefly looked over his shoulder.

He saw Vader standing in the middle of the staircase, holding one of the Habiran Guards suspended in the air with the Force while continuing to duel the other.

For a brief moment, their eyes met.

Then the senator disappeared inside the building.

The KDF personnel hurried Trotz down the corridors of the capital building and into a secured room. The chamber had been designated for emergencies and included an exit leading to an open outdoor area large enough to accommodate evacuation transports.

The senator was still trying to process what he had witnessed.

A KDF soldier turned toward him.

“Senator, we don’t have much time. We need to authorize an emergency evacuation immediately.”

Trotz looked at him, still shaken.

“Yes. Yes, of course. Call it in.”

The soldier immediately activated his communications device.

“Command, this is Senatorial Security. Initiating emergency evacuation protocol. Prepare the extraction site.”

Outside, KDF personnel began deploying throughout the surrounding area. Defensive positions were established, vehicles were moved into place, and soldiers took up positions around the evacuation perimeter.

The Empire was advancing, and the Kalyph Defense Force was preparing to resist.

Trotz watched as his personnel worked frantically to organize the evacuation. He knew the situation was deteriorating, but he had little idea just how quickly it was happening.

Then came the sound of blaster fire from somewhere down the corridor.

A soldier turned toward the entrance.

“Did you hear that?”

Before anyone could respond, another burst of blaster fire echoed through the building.

Vader was coming.

The evacuation transports arrived shortly afterward.

KDF personnel began coordinating the extraction, directing troops and preparing to move the senator.

Trotz was escorted toward the exit by several soldiers. They had barely reached the outdoor area when a transmission came through their communications channel.

“Evacuation site, this is perimeter command! Imperials have breached our positions! I repeat, Imperials have breached the evacuation site!”

The KDF soldiers immediately raised their weapons.

“Where?” one of them shouted into his communicator.

The response was interrupted by another transmission.

“WE’RE LOSING CONTROL OF THE EVACUATION SITE! I REPEAT, WE’RE LOSING CONTROL!”

Trotz stepped outside.

What he saw was chaos.

Imperial stormtroopers were pushing through the KDF defensive perimeter, exchanging blaster fire with defenders who were desperately attempting to hold their positions.

One of the hovering transports attempted to lift off, but a missile struck its side.

The craft exploded, crashing into the evacuation area and sending debris scattering across the ground.

Other transports attempted to escape, but Imperial fire quickly brought them down or forced them to abandon their approach.

“WE’VE LOST THE EVACUATION SITE! ALL UNITS, FALL BACK! FALL BACK!”

The transmission ended.

Trotz stared at the destruction around him. The evacuation had failed. His soldiers were being overwhelmed, and there was no longer any realistic possibility of getting him off the planet.

He turned toward the KDF personnel surrounding him.

“Senator, we need to move!” one of them shouted.

Trotz shook his head.

“No. Look around us.”

“Sir?”

“We’re not getting out of here.”

The soldier looked toward the advancing Imperial forces before turning back to the senator.

Trotz took a breath.

“Get into position. We’re holding this ground.”

“Senator, we can still—”

“That’s an order.”

The KDF soldiers exchanged brief glances before taking up defensive positions around him.

They would make their final stand.

The fighting continued.

Imperial stormtroopers advanced through the evacuation site, systematically eliminating the remaining KDF resistance. The defenders fought desperately, but their positions were collapsing one after another.

Then, through the smoke and blaster fire, a familiar figure emerged from the capital building.

Darth Vader.

He walked steadily toward the senator’s position, his lightsaber cutting through anyone foolish enough to stand in his way.

KDF soldiers opened fire.

Vader deflected their shots, sending blaster bolts back into the advancing defenders. One soldier fell, then another. The remaining troops attempted to reorganize their defensive line, but Vader continued advancing without hesitation.

Trotz watched as the soldiers protecting him were overwhelmed.

One of them fell only a few meters away.

Another was struck down before he could raise his weapon.

The senator looked around.

There was no escape.

A KDF soldier beside him reached for his sidearm and offered it to Trotz.

“Senator. Take this.”

Trotz accepted the blaster pistol.

Vader continued approaching.

The senator raised the weapon and fired.

Vader effortlessly deflected the bolt with his lightsaber. The redirected shot struck Trotz in the arm.

He cried out in pain, dropping the pistol as he stumbled backward. His other hand immediately moved to clutch the wound.

Vader stopped directly in front of him.

The senator looked up.

“Where is General Kota?” Vader demanded.

Trotz struggled to catch his breath.

He looked at the Dark Lord of the Sith, then past him at the destruction surrounding them.

Despite everything, he managed a faint smile.

“You’ll never find him,” he said. “He’ll find you.”

Vader stared at him.

“I hope he does.”

The Dark Lord raised his hand.

Trotz was lifted from the ground once again, his body suspended as Vader tightened his grip around the senator’s throat.

The senator struggled briefly, but there was nothing he could do.

His resistance slowly faded.

A moment later, his body went limp.

Vader released him.

Senator Corin Trotz fell lifelessly to the ground.

The Dark Lord turned away, his mechanical breathing the only sound accompanying the advancing Imperial forces.

Behind him, the Kalyph Defense Force’s resistance was collapsing.

The Empire had come looking for a Jedi General.

It would leave with control of an entire world.

Kalyph had fallen.
