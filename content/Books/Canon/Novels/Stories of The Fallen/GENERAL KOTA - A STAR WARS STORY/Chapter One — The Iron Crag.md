# Chapter One: The Iron Crag

## Scene 1 – The Fortress

The Iron Crag had been standing long before the Republic arrived.

The ancient volcanic crater rose from the blackened plains of Kalyph like the remains of some dead god. Its walls were jagged and uneven, their dark stone scarred by centuries of wind, rain, and war. Great sections of the crater had been carved away and replaced with durasteel, reinforced transparisteel, landing platforms, shield generators, and enormous blast doors.

But the Republic had never truly built the Iron Crag.

It had simply made the mountain larger.

From a distance, the fortress appeared almost impossible to comprehend. Towers of durasteel rose from the crater walls, connected by bridges wide enough to carry armored columns. Communications arrays stretched toward the sky, surrounded by rotating sensor dishes and defensive turbolaser emplacements. Gunships moved constantly between landing platforms, their engines echoing against the walls of the crater.

Thousands of lights burned throughout the fortress.

Some belonged to barracks.

Others illuminated hospitals, maintenance bays, command centers, armories, mess halls, vehicle depots, ammunition stores, communications stations, training grounds, and the countless corridors that connected them all.

The Iron Crag never slept.

It could not afford to.

For years, the fortress had been the center of General Rahm Kota’s campaign against the Separatists. Soldiers had arrived here wounded and returned to the front. Clones had marched through its gates as replacements and left as veterans. Mechanics had worked through entire nights repairing vehicles damaged in battle. Medics had learned to recognize the sound of incoming gunships before they ever saw them.

The fortress had grown with the war.

Every victory had added another hangar.

Every defeat had added another bunker.

Every battle had left another scar.

Deep within the mountain, entire battalions lived beneath layers of rock and durasteel.

The clone barracks occupied several enormous chambers carved directly into the crater’s interior. Long rows of identical bunks stretched beneath harsh white lighting, separated by equipment lockers, weapons racks, and narrow passageways. Thousands of white-armored soldiers could be housed within the fortress at any given time.

Beyond them were the militia camps.

They were different.

The volunteers of the 108th Republic Liberty Corps had built their own identity inside the fortress. Their quarters were less uniform, their equipment more varied, and their armor bore the marks of countless worlds and campaigns. Republic Army equipment stood beside locally manufactured weapons. Civilian transports were parked beside military vehicles. Some soldiers slept in proper barracks while others preferred canvas shelters erected inside enormous chambers.

The Iron Crag had room for all of them.

That was its greatest strength.

It was not merely a fortress.

It was a military city.

And the city had a harbor.

The northern face of the crater had been transformed into an enormous orbital docking complex.

Massive blast doors opened into the mountain, revealing hangars large enough to swallow entire squadrons of starfighters. Landing platforms extended from the crater walls, reinforced by enormous structural supports that disappeared into the rock below.

At the center of it all rested the fortress’s flagship.

A Venator-class Star Destroyer sat partially enclosed within the largest docking bay, its enormous hull extending beyond the mountain like the blade of some colossal weapon. Maintenance crews moved across its surface in swarms. Cranes lifted components into its hangars. Fuel lines connected its engines to the fortress below.

Beside it rested an Acclamator-class assault ship.

Two Arquitens-class command cruisers occupied smaller docking platforms farther along the crater wall, their crews constantly moving between ship and fortress.

And beneath them, secured within reinforced docking chambers, were two modified Pelta-class frigates.

Five capital ships.

All docked at the same fortress.

And the Iron Crag still had room for more.

The sound of the fortress was constant.

The deep roar of starship engines.

The clatter of armored boots against durasteel floors.

The hydraulic hiss of blast doors.

The distant thunder of artillery tests.

The shouted orders of officers.

The hum of generators buried beneath the mountain.

The voices of thousands of soldiers moving through the corridors.

Somewhere, a warning siren sounded.

Nobody stopped.

They had heard it before.

A squad of clone troopers marched beneath a massive Republic banner, their armor scratched and scorched from their latest engagement. A group of militia soldiers passed them going the opposite direction, carrying crates of ammunition toward a waiting transport.

Medics rushed a wounded clone through a corridor.

Mechanics argued over a damaged repulsorlift.

A communications officer ran past with a datapad clutched beneath his arm.

High above the main hangar, a lone figure occasionally passed behind the transparisteel windows of the command levels. General Rahm Kota was the only Jedi stationed within the Iron Crag.

Everywhere, the same thing.

Movement.

Work.

War.

The Iron Crag absorbed all of it.

It had become something more than stone and steel.

It was a fortress that remembered.

Every scar across its walls told a story.

Every blast door had been sealed during a battle.

Every hospital bed had held someone who had believed they would never see home again.

Every barracks had contained soldiers who had left through the gates and never returned.

And at the center of it all was the man who had made the Iron Crag his home.

General Rahm Kota.

To the soldiers stationed there, the fortress was more than a headquarters.

It was the place they returned to.

It was where they were healed.

Where they were resupplied.

Where they trained.

Where they mourned.

Where they prepared to go back to war.

The Iron Crag was their shield.

And if the Separatists ever came for it, they would discover exactly how difficult it was to break.

  

Deep within the fortress, far from the hangars and barracks, a set of enormous blast doors opened.

Beyond them was the command center.

The room was built into the heart of the mountain.

A massive holographic table dominated its center, projecting the Kalyph system in blue light. Dozens of smaller displays surrounded it, each showing troop movements, supply routes, communications traffic, planetary maps, or intelligence reports.

Officers stood around the table in conversation.

Clone troopers occupied several positions around the room.

Militia commanders waited near the far wall.

The air was thick with the quiet tension of men who had spent too long at war to ever truly relax.

At the head of the table stood General Rahm Kota.

The fortress continued moving around him.

But here, everything waited for his next order.

## Scene 2 – The Briefing

The tactical display shifted.

The enormous holographic projection of Kalyph disappeared, replaced by a detailed map of the planet’s eastern hemisphere.

Kota stood at the head of the table, arms folded across his chest.

Around him sat the men and women responsible for keeping the Iron Crag—and the war around it—alive.

Commander Vantage stood closest to the display.

The clone commander watched the map without speaking.

Beside him, Major Tally was already working through a datapad.

General Jarek Turnmaash leaned back in his chair, arms crossed.

Brigadier General Jaxen Kel-Zor sat beside him, his enormous cybernetic frame making the chair look almost too small.

Across the table, General Toren Trowk studied the map with quiet concentration.

Tala Vess’undri stood slightly apart from the others, her eyes moving between the tactical display and the intelligence reports in her hand.

Dr. Bor Regg sat near the end of the table, hands folded calmly in front of him.

Grissom Thruun’ka had somehow managed to bring a datapad nearly the size of a small serving tray.

Captain Drenn Craggor stood near the entrance.

He had not sat down.

He rarely did.

Kota looked at the display.

“Oros.”

The hologram zoomed toward the capital city.

“What’s changed?”

Tala answered first.

“The Separatists have increased their activity around the city.”

Kota looked at her.

“How?”

“Reconnaissance. Sabotage. Small arms shipments. Communications interference. Nothing large enough to justify a full-scale assault.”

Kel-Zor leaned forward.

“Yet.”

Tala glanced at him.

“Yet.”

The massive cyborg tapped one finger against the table.

“Then reinforce Oros.”

Trowk didn’t look up.

“No.”

Kel-Zor turned toward him.

“No?”

“We reinforce Oros, we tell them exactly what we think they’re doing.”

Kel-Zor gave a humorless smile.

“General, if they’re about to attack the capital, I don’t particularly care what they know we think.”

“They may not be attacking the capital.”

“Then why are they moving weapons into the surrounding districts?”

“Because they want us to believe they’re attacking the capital.”

Kel-Zor stared at him for a moment.

“You’ve been waiting all morning to say that, haven’t you?”

Trowk finally looked up.

“Since breakfast.”

Jarek chuckled.

“At least somebody’s having a good morning.”

Kota’s eyes remained on the map.

“Vantage.”

The clone commander stepped forward.

“I want more reconnaissance.”

Kel-Zor sighed.

“Of course you do.”

Vantage ignored him.

“We’re seeing too many gaps in our intelligence. Separatist units are moving through areas where our patrols aren’t operating. We don’t know their numbers, their commanders, or their objective.”

Tala nodded.

“That’s the problem.”

She touched her datapad.

Several red indicators appeared around Oros.

“They aren’t behaving like a conventional army.”

Kota looked at her.

“Explain.”

“The attacks are too small.”

She enlarged the display.

“Three attacks on supply convoys. Two communications stations sabotaged. Four attempted weapons transfers. One attack against a Republic patrol.”

Tally finally looked up from his datapad.

“That’s ten incidents.”

“Eleven.”

Tally frowned.

Tala pointed to another location.

“This one hasn’t been officially confirmed.”

Tally immediately began entering information.

“Then it isn’t eleven.”

Tala raised an eyebrow.

“Someone was shot.”

“That doesn’t make it an official engagement.”

“It makes it an engagement.”

“Not until the report is filed.”

Jarek looked between them.

“Does anyone else find it comforting that our intelligence officer and our logistics officer can argue about paperwork while the Separatists are shooting people?”

Grissom looked up from his datapad.

“Paperwork matters.”

Jarek smiled.

“Of course you’d say that.”

Grissom shrugged.

“Someone has to know where everything is.”

Kel-Zor pointed toward him.

“Tell him.”

Grissom looked at Kota.

“If General Kel-Zor wants to reinforce Oros, I need three additional freighters.”

Kel-Zor blinked.

“Three?”

“Four would be better.”

“I asked for reinforcements, not a fleet.”

“You asked for a reinforced brigade.”

“Yes.”

“Then I need the freighters.”

Kel-Zor leaned back.

“How much equipment do we have?”

Grissom stared at him.

“Enough for what we currently have.”

“And if we deploy?”

“Then we have less.”

Kel-Zor sighed.

“You’re impossible.”

Grissom smiled pleasantly.

“Thank you, General.”

Bor Regg quietly cleared his throat.

“If we’re discussing the possibility of a major engagement, I should point out that the hospital is nearly empty.”

Everyone turned toward him.

Bor continued calmly.

“We have enough beds, surgical equipment, bacta, and medical personnel to support a large engagement.”

Jarek frowned.

“That’s supposed to make me feel better?”

“No.”

Bor folded his hands.

“It’s supposed to make you aware that we’re prepared.”

Kota glanced toward him.

“How long can you maintain that?”

“Depends on how many people you send me.”

Kel-Zor gave a small grin.

“That’s the first thing you’ve said today that I like.”

Bor looked at him.

“I wasn’t complimenting you.”

The room went quiet for a moment.

Then Jarek laughed.

Even Vantage allowed the smallest smile.

Kota didn’t.

But his expression softened.

Only slightly.

He turned back toward the map.

“Craggor.”

The garrison commander stepped closer.

“The Iron Crag is secure.”

Kota waited.

Craggor knew what he meant.

“I recommend increasing readiness.”

Kel-Zor frowned.

“Against what?”

“Anything.”

Kel-Zor looked at him.

“That’s helpful.”

Craggor’s expression didn’t change.

“The Separatists know the Iron Crag is the center of our operations. If they’re probing Oros, they may be trying to draw our forces away from here.”

Trowk nodded.

“Which supports my theory.”

Craggor continued.

“If they’re planning an attack on the fortress, they’ll want as much of the garrison deployed as possible.”

Kota finally looked at him.

“What do you need?”

“Nothing dramatic.”

Craggor gestured toward the display.

“Increase patrols around the outer perimeter. Keep the defensive batteries manned. Lock down nonessential access points. Put the reserve companies on standby.”

Kota nodded once.

“Do it.”

“Yes, General.”

Vantage studied the map.

“There’s another possibility.”

Kota looked at him.

Vantage pointed toward a region south of Oros.

“They aren’t trying to take the city.”

Tala turned toward him.

“What are they trying to do?”

Vantage zoomed the display outward.

“Make us look there.”

The room fell silent.

Kel-Zor leaned forward.

“And where would you look?”

Vantage pointed toward the southern supply routes.

“Here.”

Tally immediately began checking the logistical data.

“Those routes supply nearly half the eastern theater.”

Grissom’s expression changed.

“That’s a problem.”

Jarek looked at him.

“How bad?”

Grissom tapped his datapad.

“If those routes are compromised, we lose food, ammunition, medical supplies, spare parts—”

“How long?”

Grissom looked at Kota.

“Three weeks before it becomes uncomfortable.”

Kel-Zor snorted.

“Uncomfortable?”

Grissom shrugged.

“After that, it becomes significantly more uncomfortable.”

Jarek laughed.

Trowk didn’t.

“Then we should move a brigade south.”

Kel-Zor immediately shook his head.

“Which brigade?”

“Mine.”

“You’d take the entire Second Expeditionary Brigade?”

“If necessary.”

Kel-Zor leaned across the table.

“That’s exactly what they’d want.”

Trowk looked at him.

“And sitting here is exactly what they’d want if I’m right.”

The two generals stared at one another.

Kota finally spoke.

“Enough.”

The room went silent.

Kota looked around the table.

“Tala.”

“Yes, General?”

“What do you believe?”

Tala paused.

“I believe the Separatists are testing us.”

“Testing what?”

“Our response time. Our communications. Our patrol patterns. Our command structure.”

She looked at the map.

“They’re trying to learn how we react before they commit to anything.”

Kota nodded.

“Vantage?”

“I agree.”

“Kel-Zor?”

The cyborg crossed his arms.

“I think we’re giving them too much credit.”

Kota raised an eyebrow.

Kel-Zor continued.

“They’re Separatists. They attack where we’re weak. We reinforce the weak point and break them.”

Trowk shook his head.

“That’s exactly how you get baited.”

Kel-Zor smiled.

“That’s why I have you around.”

Trowk sighed.

Jarek leaned forward.

“I think they’re watching Oros.”

Kota looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because Oros matters.”

Jarek gestured toward the map.

“It’s the capital. It’s where the politicians sit. Where the civilians live. Where the news crews go.”

He looked around the table.

“If I’m trying to make the Republic look weak, I don’t attack a supply depot.”

He pointed toward Oros.

“I attack here.”

Kota considered that.

Bor spoke quietly.

“And if they’re right?”

Everyone looked at him.

He gestured toward the city.

“Then there are millions of civilians between us and the battlefield.”

The room became quiet again.

Kota stared at the hologram.

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then Kota stepped closer to the display.

The capital.

The supply routes.

The surrounding cities.

The Iron Crag.

Everything was connected.

“Here’s what we know.”

He pointed toward Oros.

“They’re active.”

Then toward the southern routes.

“They’re moving.”

Then toward the fortress.

“And they’re watching us.”

Kota looked around the room.

“We don’t know where they’ll strike.”

His eyes settled on each officer in turn.

“Until we do, every road, every city, every depot, and every fortress is a target.”

Nobody interrupted.

“We increase readiness across the entire theater.”

Kota pointed toward Vantage.

“More reconnaissance.”

Vantage nodded.

“Yes, General.”

“Tally.”

“Already working on troop movements.”

“Jarek.”

“Second and third militia companies will be ready.”

“Kel-Zor.”

The cyborg straightened.

“First Brigade remains on standby.”

“Trowk.”

“I’ll prepare defensive contingencies for the southern routes.”

“Tala.”

“More intelligence.”

“Bor.”

“Medical teams will be ready.”

“Grissom.”

The quartermaster sighed.

“I’ll find your freighters.”

Kota looked at Craggor.

“Raise the Iron Crag’s readiness.”

Craggor nodded.

“Understood.”

Kota looked once more at the map.

“Nobody moves a brigade until we know why.”

He turned away from the table.

“Dismissed.”

Chairs moved.

Datapads activated.

Orders began flowing almost immediately.

The room came alive again.

Kota remained where he was.

For a moment, he stared at the holographic image of Oros.

Then he quietly said,

“Something’s coming.”

No one heard him.

And for now, Kota had no idea how close he was to being right.

## Scene 3 – The Tour of the Iron Crag

### Stop 1 – The Hospital

The command center doors closed behind Kota.

For the first time since the briefing began, the general was alone.

Not truly alone.

The Iron Crag never allowed that.

The corridors were filled with the sounds of boots against durasteel, distant machinery, and the muffled voices of soldiers carrying out the orders that had just been given. Technicians moved quickly between workstations. Clone troopers passed in small groups, their armor still marked by the remnants of recent battles.

Kota walked through them without slowing.

Several soldiers nodded as he passed.

He returned each gesture.

He knew most of their faces.

Some of their names.

Others he had learned to recognize by their armor markings, their voices, or the way they carried themselves.

He turned down a long corridor and approached a set of sliding doors.

A blue medical insignia was displayed above them.

The doors opened.

The smell of disinfectant met him first.

The hospital was quieter than the rest of the fortress.

Rows of medical beds stretched across the main ward, separated by curtains and small equipment stations. Medical droids moved silently between them. Nurses and orderlies walked from station to station, checking equipment and reviewing datapads.

There were empty beds.

A lot of them.

Kota stopped just inside the entrance.

Dr. Bor Regg was standing over one of the occupied beds.

The Mon Calamari physician looked up.

“General.”

Kota nodded.

“Doctor.”

Bor glanced toward the empty beds.

“You’re here to make sure we’re still useless.”

Kota frowned.

“What?”

Bor gestured around the room.

“Nearly empty.”

Kota looked at the ward.

“That’s good.”

“It is.”

Bor picked up a datapad.

“I’d prefer it that way.”

Kota walked farther into the room.

“How many?”

“Four.”

Kota stopped.

“Four?”

“Four.”

Bor gave him a tired smile.

“Don’t look so disappointed.”

“I’m not.”

“You have the expression.”

“I always have this expression.”

Bor laughed quietly.

“Fair enough.”

Kota approached the first bed.

A militia sergeant lay beneath a thin medical blanket, his left arm immobilized in a sling. A bandage wrapped around his shoulder and upper chest.

The sergeant noticed Kota.

He immediately tried to sit up.

“General.”

Kota raised a hand.

“Stay down.”

The sergeant hesitated.

Then obeyed.

Kota looked at the medical readout.

“Supply convoy?”

“Yes, sir.”

“What happened?”

The sergeant grimaced.

“Ambush.”

“How many?”

“Six attackers. Maybe eight.”

“Maybe?”

“It was dark.”

Kota nodded.

“Casualties?”

“None dead.”

Bor spoke from behind him.

“Three wounded. He’s the worst.”

The sergeant looked toward the doctor.

“I’ve been told I’m lucky.”

“You are.”

“Doesn’t feel like it.”

Bor smiled.

“That’s because you were shot.”

The sergeant gave a quiet laugh.

Kota looked back at him.

“How long until you’re back on duty?”

Bor answered before the sergeant could.

“He’s not.”

The sergeant looked offended.

“Doctor—”

“Two weeks minimum.”

“I can walk.”

“Wonderful.”

“I can shoot.”

“Even better.”

“I can carry equipment.”

Bor folded his arms.

“You can also bleed all over my floor.”

The sergeant looked at Kota.

“General?”

Kota shook his head.

“Listen to the doctor.”

The sergeant sighed.

“Yes, sir.”

Kota moved on.

The next two beds belonged to militia privates.

They had been wounded during a patrol, both suffering blaster injuries that had been treated quickly. One had a bandaged leg. The other had a burn across his side.

The first private noticed Kota approaching.

“General.”

Kota looked at his leg.

“How bad?”

“Doctor says I’ll walk.”

Bor nodded.

“He will.”

The private smiled.

“See?”

Kota looked at Bor.

“How long?”

“Three days before he’s walking without assistance.”

The private’s smile faded.

“Three days?”

“Unless you’d like to lose the leg.”

“No, sir.”

“Then three days.”

Kota moved to the other private.

“What’s your name?”

The young man looked surprised.

“Volsen, General.”

Kota nodded.

“Where were you hit?”

“South patrol route.”

“Same place as the convoy?”

“Different route.”

Kota’s expression tightened.

“How many attackers?”

“Four.”

“Vehicles?”

“No.”

“Uniforms?”

“No.”

Kota looked toward Bor.

The doctor said nothing.

Kota looked back at the private.

“Did you see their faces?”

“No, sir.”

“Anything unusual?”

The private thought for a moment.

“They knew where we were going.”

Kota’s eyes narrowed.

“How?”

“I don’t know.”

Kota nodded.

“Neither do I.”

He moved on.

The fourth bed was tucked slightly farther from the others.

A clone corporal lay beneath the medical lights.

His armor was stacked neatly beside the bed.

His helmet sat on top of it.

Kota stopped.

The clone was awake.

He looked toward the general.

“Sir.”

Kota looked at the medical readout.

“Corporal CT-—”

He stopped.

Then looked at the clone.

“What’s your name?”

The clone hesitated.

“CT-4472, sir.”

Kota shook his head.

“Your name.”

The clone stared at him for a moment.

“Vek, sir.”

Kota nodded.

“Vek.”

“Yes, sir.”

“What happened?”

“Patrol.”

Kota looked at the wound.

“Blaster?”

“Yes, sir.”

“How close?”

Vek thought about it.

“Close enough.”

Bor looked over.

“The shot passed through the armor and clipped the shoulder. He’s lucky.”

Kota looked at the clone.

“How do you feel?”

“Like I’ve been shot.”

Bor smiled.

“He’s been making jokes since he woke up.”

Vek looked at the doctor.

“Helps.”

Kota pulled a chair closer to the bed.

He sat.

Vek seemed surprised.

Kota didn’t seem to notice.

“How many were with you?”

“Four.”

“Everyone else?”

“Fine.”

Kota nodded.

“Good.”

For a moment, neither spoke.

Kota looked at Vek’s armor.

The white plates were scratched and stained from the patrol.

“You keep your armor clean.”

Vek glanced toward it.

“Commander Vantage says dirty armor makes dirty soldiers.”

Kota almost smiled.

“Vantage says a lot of things.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Most of them useful.”

Vek smiled.

Kota stood.

“Get some rest.”

“Yes, General.”

Kota took a few steps before stopping.

He looked back.

“Vek.”

The clone looked at him.

“You did well.”

The corporal’s expression changed.

For just a moment, the soldier looked less like one of thousands of identical white-armored clones and more like an individual who had been seen.

“Thank you, General.”

Kota nodded.

Then he walked away.

Bor followed him toward the exit.

For several seconds, neither spoke.

When they reached the doors, Bor finally said,

“You know, most generals don’t visit their wounded personally.”

Kota kept walking.

“Most generals don’t command my soldiers.”

Bor smiled faintly.

“That’s true.”

The doors opened.

The noise of the fortress returned.

Kota stopped just outside.

Bor stood beside him.

“General.”

Kota looked at him.

Bor’s expression had become more serious.

“We’re fine now.”

Kota waited.

“But if the fighting around Oros gets worse…”

He glanced back through the doors toward the hospital.

“We won’t stay this empty.”

Kota looked into the ward.

At the four wounded soldiers.

At the rows of empty beds.

At the medical droids waiting silently beside them.

He understood.

“I know.”

Bor nodded.

Kota turned toward the corridor.

“Keep them alive.”

Bor gave him a quiet smile.

“That’s what I do.”

Kota continued down the corridor.

Behind him, the hospital doors closed.

The Iron Crag kept moving.

## Stop 2 – The Vehicle Maintenance Hangar

The hospital doors closed behind Kota.

The quiet of the medical ward disappeared almost immediately.

The farther he walked through the Iron Crag, the louder the fortress became.

Boots struck durasteel.

Technicians shouted instructions across corridors.

Hydraulic systems hissed behind reinforced walls.

Somewhere beneath his feet, enormous machinery rumbled to life.

Kota followed the sound.

He turned a corner and entered one of the fortress’s largest internal chambers.

The vehicle maintenance hangar.

The chamber stretched farther than Kota could see from the entrance.

Several levels of maintenance platforms surrounded the central floor, connected by walkways, lifts, and suspended gantries. Republic vehicles occupied nearly every available bay.

AT-RTs stood on elevated platforms with their armor panels removed.

Speeder bikes waited in rows.

Armored transports were suspended above the floor while mechanics worked beneath them.

A pair of artillery pieces were being stripped down near the far wall.

Everywhere Kota looked, someone was working.

The hangar had the organized chaos of a battlefield that had somehow been brought indoors.

Mechanics moved between vehicles carrying tools and replacement parts.

Droids transported heavy components across the floor.

Crates of spare equipment were stacked against the walls.

A damaged speeder rolled past Kota with half its engine exposed.

The driver noticed him.

“General!”

Kota gave him a short nod.

The speeder continued on.

Kota walked farther into the hangar.

A familiar voice reached him through the noise.

“You’re holding it wrong.”

Another voice answered.

“I’ve been holding it this way for five minutes.”

“Exactly.”

Kota stopped.

He recognized the first voice.

Ash.

He followed it toward the center of the hangar.

A Republic infantry transport sat elevated above the maintenance floor.

Its rear section had been opened, exposing the drive system.

Three mechanics were working around it.

One was a veteran clone mechanic wearing a grease-stained work uniform.

The other two were Ash and Novek Volsign.

Ash had somehow managed to get grease across nearly every exposed part of his armor.

Novek was lying beneath the vehicle with a hydrospanner in one hand.

The clone mechanic was holding a datapad and looking increasingly irritated.

“Give me the stabilizer.”

Ash handed him a component.

The mechanic stared at it.

“That’s a coolant regulator.”

Ash looked down at his empty hand.

“Oh.”

Novek’s voice came from beneath the vehicle.

“I told you.”

Ash looked toward him.

“You said I was holding the wrong part.”

“That is what I said.”

“You could’ve told me which one was right.”

“You’ve been working on the same vehicle for twenty minutes.”

Ash shrugged.

“I was getting there.”

The mechanic closed his eyes for a moment.

“General.”

Kota stepped closer.

The mechanic straightened.

“General Kota.”

Kota looked at the vehicle.

“Problem?”

The mechanic glanced toward Ash and Novek.

“Yes.”

Ash immediately shook his head.

“No.”

Novek slid out from beneath the transport.

“Maybe.”

The mechanic pointed at him.

“That’s the problem.”

Kota looked between them.

“What happened?”

The mechanic gestured toward the exposed drive system.

“I was replacing the stabilizer.”

Ash nodded.

“We’re helping.”

Novek added, “He’s learning.”

Ash pointed toward Novek.

“I’m learning.”

Novek wiped his hands on a cloth.

“I’m keeping him from breaking anything.”

The mechanic stared at them.

“General, I’d appreciate it if they’d stop helping.”

Ash looked offended.

“We haven’t broken anything.”

The mechanic looked toward the floor.

A small pile of discarded parts sat beside him.

Ash followed his gaze.

“Those were already broken.”

Novek laughed.

“They were.”

The mechanic looked at Novek.

“Thank you.”

“You’re welcome.”

Ash frowned.

“Whose side are you on?”

“The side of the person who knows what he’s doing.”

Ash looked at Kota.

“General?”

Kota studied the three of them.

Then the vehicle.

Then the pile of parts.

“How bad?”

The mechanic answered immediately.

“The vehicle?”

“Yes.”

“Fine.”

“And them?”

The mechanic sighed.

“That’s more complicated.”

Novek laughed again.

Kota looked at him.

“You’re Volsign.”

“Private Novek Volsign, sir.”

“Former mechanic.”

Novek nodded.

“Yes, sir.”

“Where’d you learn?”

“Back home.”

Kota knew there was more to the answer.

He didn’t ask.

Novek’s hometown had been destroyed by the Separatists.

He had joined the 108th afterward.

Kota had seen plenty of volunteers arrive at the Iron Crag with the same story.

Some wanted revenge.

Some wanted justice.

Some simply wanted to make sure what happened to them didn’t happen to someone else.

Novek seemed to be one of the last.

Kota looked toward Ash.

“And you?”

Ash stood a little straighter.

“Sergeant Ash, CT-4489.”

Kota nodded.

“Ash.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Since when are you a mechanic?”

Ash smiled.

“I’m not.”

The veteran mechanic immediately pointed at him.

“Exactly.”

Ash continued.

“I’m learning.”

Kota looked at the mechanic.

“How long have you been teaching him?”

“Long enough to regret it.”

Ash laughed.

Novek shook his head.

The mechanic finally allowed himself a smile.

“I’ve been working on Republic vehicles since the beginning of the war.”

Kota nodded.

“That’s more like it.”

The mechanic returned to the transport.

“Now, if everyone is finished distracting me, I’d like to get this thing running.”

Ash immediately picked up a tool.

“What do you need?”

The mechanic pointed at him.

“Put that down.”

Ash slowly lowered it.

“I was just asking.”

“I know.”

Novek reached for another tool.

The mechanic looked at him.

“You too.”

Novek stopped.

“Fine.”

Kota watched them.

There was something almost strange about the scene.

The clone and the volunteer were working side by side without thinking about the difference between them.

Ash was curious.

Novek was patient.

The veteran mechanic was irritated with both.

It was not the sort of thing that would appear in a Republic report.

No officer would write down that a clone sergeant had spent his afternoon learning how to repair a transmission from a civilian volunteer.

But it mattered.

Kota knew that.

The Republic was built on people like these.

Not speeches.

Not legislation.

People.

Ash leaned toward Novek.

“What’s this part do?”

Novek looked at it.

“Controls the stabilizer.”

Ash nodded.

“Right.”

“You already knew that.”

“I was testing you.”

Novek stared at him.

“You’re terrible at lying.”

Ash grinned.

The mechanic looked toward Kota.

“See what I deal with?”

Kota’s mouth twitched.

“Seems manageable.”

The mechanic gave him a look.

“You don’t have to work with them.”

Kota looked at Ash.

Then Novek.

“True.”

Ash smiled.

“See?”

Kota continued walking around the vehicle.

“What happened to it?”

The mechanic answered.

“Drive-system failure during the last patrol.”

“Combat damage?”

“No.”

“Then?”

“Overheating.”

Novek looked up.

“Because the coolant regulator was damaged.”

The mechanic nodded.

“Correct.”

Ash pointed at him.

“I knew that.”

Novek smiled.

“You didn’t.”

“I was going to.”

“You were holding the wrong part.”

Ash looked at the mechanic.

“Why does everyone keep bringing that up?”

The mechanic went back to work.

“Because you keep proving them right.”

Kota looked toward the far side of the hangar.

A squad of clone mechanics was repairing an AT-RT.

Another group worked on a damaged armored transport.

A militia soldier and a clone were carrying the same heavy component together.

No one seemed to think anything of it.

That was important.

Years of war had created relationships that the Republic’s bureaucracy would never have anticipated.

Clones and volunteers fought together.

They ate together.

They trained together.

And apparently, they argued over vehicle parts together.

Kota looked back toward Ash and Novek.

Ash was holding a tool again.

The mechanic hadn’t noticed yet.

Kota raised an eyebrow.

“Didn’t he tell you to put that down?”

Ash looked at the tool.

Then at Kota.

“Yes, sir.”

“Then why are you holding it?”

Ash slowly placed it on the workbench.

“Forgot.”

Novek laughed.

The mechanic shook his head.

“General, please take him.”

Kota folded his arms.

“Why?”

“Because I need to finish this.”

Ash looked at the vehicle.

“I can stay.”

“No.”

“I can help.”

“No.”

“I’ve learned a lot.”

“That’s what worries me.”

Novek laughed so hard that he had to turn away.

Ash looked at him.

“You aren’t helping.”

“I know.”

“Novek, hand me the replacement stabilizer.”

Novek picked up the correct component.

“Here.”

Then he looked at Ash.

“And don’t touch anything.”

“Understood.”

Kota shook his head.

He looked toward the exit.

“I’ve got another stop.”

“Where?”

Novek nodded.

“We’ll stay here.”

“You’re sure?”

“We promised him we’d help install the stabilizer.”

“That was before I knew what ‘help’ meant.”

“I can hold it.”

“No.”

“I wasn’t going to do anything.”

“Hey, Novek.”

“Do you think the General actually trusts us?”

Then Novek answered.

“I think he’d trust us more if you stopped touching things.”

Ash laughed.

The doors closed.

The noise of the hangar faded behind Kota.

Ahead of him, the corridor led toward the communications center.

Kota continued walking.

## Scene 3 – The Communications Center

The corridors became quieter as Kota left the maintenance hangar.

Not silent.

The Iron Crag was never silent.

But the heavy machinery and constant shouting of the maintenance crews gradually faded behind him, replaced by the steady hum of communications equipment.

Kota turned down another corridor.

Two clone troopers stepped aside as he approached.

“General.”

Kota gave them a short nod.

Ahead, a set of reinforced doors opened automatically.

The communications center occupied one of the upper levels of the fortress. From here, the Iron Crag’s internal communications network stretched in every direction, carrying information between the fortress, the ships in its docks, patrols in the field, and military installations across Kalyph.

Banks of terminals filled the room.

Operators sat at their stations, monitoring frequencies from across the system. Holographic displays showed reconnaissance flights, patrol routes, supply convoys, military units, and long-range transmissions.

Dozens of conversations took place at once.

“Recon Flight Aurek-Seven, report.”

“Signal strength dropping.”

“Try the secondary frequency.”

“Negative. Still nothing.”

“Send it through the relay.”

Kota stepped inside.

Several officers noticed him.

The room became slightly quieter.

A clone captain approached from the central communications station.

“General.”

Kota looked toward the main display.

“Reconnaissance.”

The captain glanced at his datapad.

“Most of the flights are reporting normally.”

“Most?”

The captain hesitated.

“We’re having some interference.”

“What kind?”

“Unusual.”

Kota waited.

The captain looked slightly embarrassed.

“That’s the official technical term.”

Kota raised an eyebrow.

The captain sighed.

“Some of the long-range signals are breaking up. We’ve had intermittent interference across the eastern sector.”

“How long?”

“About twenty minutes.”

“Cause?”

“Unknown.”

Kota looked toward one of the operators.

“Can you isolate it?”

The operator shook his head.

“Not yet, General.”

The communications captain crossed his arms.

“Probably another ion storm.”

Kota looked at him.

“Another?”

“We’ve had several this week.”

“That’s not what I asked.”

The captain gave a faint smile.

“No, General.”

Before Kota could respond, Tala spoke from one of the nearby stations.

“It doesn’t look like weather.”

Kota turned.

Tala stood over a portable display, studying a stream of data.

She hadn’t followed him from the briefing.

She had simply remained at her station, doing what she always did.

Kota walked toward her.

“Why?”

Tala brought the display closer.

“The interference isn’t moving.”

The communications captain frowned.

“What do you mean?”

“An ion storm moves.”

She enlarged the affected area.

“This doesn’t.”

Several red patches appeared across the map.

“They’re staying in the same locations.”

The captain leaned closer.

“Could be sensor error.”

“Across three separate systems?”

He paused.

“No.”

Tala pointed toward another section of the display.

“And this.”

A second group of interference appeared.

“The pattern repeats.”

The captain stared at it.

“That’s not possible.”

Tala looked at him.

“It is.”

Kota studied the display.

“Can you identify it?”

“No.”

“Track it?”

“Not yet.”

Kota looked toward the communications captain.

“Reconnaissance flights?”

“Still transmitting.”

“Any reports of Separatist activity?”

“Nothing confirmed.”

Kota nodded.

“Keep looking.”

The captain straightened.

“Yes, General.”

Kota turned toward the exit.

Tala remained at her station.

She watched the interference disappear back into the shifting streams of information.

Something about it bothered her.

She couldn’t explain why.

That bothered her even more.

Kota stepped through the doors.

They closed behind him.

He took three steps.

Then stopped.

Something had changed.

Not in the corridor.

The Iron Crag continued around him.

Machines continued running.

Soldiers continued walking.

Doors opened and closed.

Someone laughed somewhere down the corridor.

A transport engine rumbled somewhere deep inside the fortress.

Everything was normal.

But Kota could no longer feel it.

He closed his eyes.

The Force had changed.

Not disappeared.

Changed.

There was no vision.

No voice.

No image of an enemy.

Nothing that told him what was coming.

Only a stillness where there should not have been stillness.

Kota reached outward.

The Force moved around him.

Through the fortress.

Through the soldiers.

Through the thousands of lives occupying the Iron Crag.

He could feel them.

The mechanics in the hangar.

The wounded in the hospital.

The officers in the communications center.

The clone troopers walking the corridors.

All of them.

And beneath it—

Something else.

Danger.

Kota’s eyes opened.

He didn’t know where it was.

He didn’t know when it would come.

He didn’t know what it would look like.

But it was there.

Approaching.

Kota turned.

Behind him, the communications center doors opened.

Ash and Novek stepped into the corridor.

Ash was still wiping grease from his hands.

“General?”

Kota didn’t answer.

Ash slowed.

Novek looked between them.

“Is he all right?”

Ash shrugged.

“I don’t know.”

They stopped several meters away.

Kota remained still.

His eyes stayed fixed down the corridor.

Ash glanced at Novek.

“Should we—”

The alarms began.

The sound tore through the fortress.

Red emergency lights ignited overhead.

Sirens echoed through the corridors.

Every conversation stopped.

Every soldier froze.

For one brief moment, the Iron Crag seemed to hold its breath.

Then the communications center exploded into activity.

Doors slammed open.

Officers shouted.

Operators rushed between stations.

Clone troopers began moving toward their assigned posts.

The communications center behind Kota filled with voices.

Kota turned.

The doors opened again.

The communications captain came running into the corridor.

His composure was gone.

“General!”

Kota faced him.

“Report.”

The captain struggled to catch his breath.

“Mass emergency transmission from Oros.”

Kota’s expression hardened.

“Put it through.”

“Yes, General.”

The captain turned back toward the communications center.

A holographic projector activated.

Static filled the corridor.

Then the image appeared.

Oros.

Smoke filled the skyline.

Buildings burned across entire districts.

Blaster fire flashed between towers.

Military transports raced through the streets.

The image shook violently as an explosion erupted somewhere beyond the camera’s view.

Nobody spoke.

The communications captain stared at the transmission.

“The planetary defense force is being overrun.”

Kota watched the burning city.

The captain continued.

“Full-scale Separatist assault.”

Another explosion rocked the hologram.

“They’re inside the city.”

Ash stared at the image.

Novek’s expression hardened.

Behind them, Tala appeared in the doorway.

She immediately began pulling up every available report.

“How many?” Kota asked.

The captain looked at the incoming data.

“Unknown.”

“Defense strength?”

“Heavy losses.”

“Command?”

“Still transmitting.”

Kota watched the city.

“How long?”

The captain swallowed.

“Minutes.”

Kota looked back toward the communications center.

The room had gone almost completely silent.

Everyone was waiting.

Not for a battle plan.

Not yet.

For orders.

Kota looked at the communications captain.

“Make a fortress-wide announcement.”

The captain straightened.

“What should it say?”

Kota’s eyes moved toward the burning image of Oros.

“All command officers are to report to the command center immediately.”

The captain nodded.

“Yes, General.”

He hurried back to his station.

Kota remained in the corridor.

A second later, every speaker in the Iron Crag came alive.

The voice of the communications captain echoed through the fortress.

“Attention, all command personnel.”

The alarms continued beneath his voice.

“All command personnel are to report to the command center immediately.”

A pause.

“This is a priority command summons.”

The announcement ended.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then the Iron Crag moved.

Doors opened throughout the fortress.

Officers emerged from offices.

Commanders abandoned their quarters.

Clone officers began moving toward the central command levels.

Militia leaders received the summons.

The fortress’s senior leadership was being called together.

Kota looked once more at Oros.

The capital was burning.

The Separatists had crossed the line.

This was no patrol skirmish.

No border raid.

No isolated attack.

It was an assault on the heart of Kalyph.

And for the first time in years, the Iron Crag was preparing to send its army into a battle large enough to consume it.

Ash stood beside Novek, staring at the hologram.

“That’s the capital.”

Novek nodded.

“Yeah.”

Ash looked toward the corridors where officers were rushing past them.

“We’re going.”

Novek looked at him.

“We don’t know that yet.”

Ash looked back at the burning city.

“Yes, we do.”

Kota heard him.

He said nothing.

He simply turned toward the command center.

The doors opened.

Kota walked through.

Behind him, the Iron Crag began gathering its commanders.

The war had come to Oros.

Now the Iron Crag would decide how to answer.
