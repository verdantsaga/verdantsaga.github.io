# Chapter Three — The Last Day of the Republic

## CHP3 Scene 1 — Departure from Oros

Inside the command post, the battle was finally beginning to give way to recovery.

Tactical displays still covered the walls, showing the positions of Republic forces throughout Oros. Most of the markers had turned blue. The remaining red signals were scattered across the outer districts, where patrols were still sweeping through damaged streets and abandoned Separatist positions.

Kota stood before the central display with his arms folded.

The capital district was secure.

The main avenues had been cleared.

The surviving Kalyph Defense Force units were reorganizing, while medical teams continued working through the wounded. Fires still burned across the skyline, but the city was no longer under immediate threat of capture.

It had cost them heavily.

Kota studied the display in silence.

Behind him, officers moved between terminals, relaying reports and updating tactical maps. The room was quieter than it had been during the battle, but nobody had relaxed. Too many soldiers were still outside. Too many wounded were still being counted.

A militia communications officer approached from one of the side consoles.

“General?”

Kota turned.

“What?”

“We have an incoming transmission from Iron Crag.”

“Put it through.”

The officer activated the command post’s central communications channel.

A moment later, General Jarek Turnmaash’s voice filled the room.

“Kota, you there?”

“I’m here.”

“Good. I’ve received word from the Senator’s people. The additional Kalyph Defense Force units and planetary assistance have arrived.”

Kota looked toward the tactical display.

“How many?”

“Enough to take over security operations. Fresh troops, fresh supplies, and enough personnel to relieve our people from the worst of the cleanup.”

Kota nodded slowly.

That was what they needed.

The 108th had been fighting almost continuously since landing on Oros. The militia brigades were scattered across multiple fronts, and even the soldiers who had survived without serious wounds were running on exhaustion.

“Can they hold the city?” Kota asked.

“According to the reports, yes. The Senator’s reinforcements are already coordinating with the local command structure.”

“Good.”

Turnmaash paused.

“Your troops have done more than enough for one day.”

Kota glanced around the command post.

No one looked rested.

“Then we’ll begin moving out.”

“I’ll notify Iron Crag and prepare for their arrival.”

“Have medical and maintenance crews ready.”

“Already doing it.”

Kota almost smiled.

“Of course you are.”

Turnmaash gave a low chuckle.

“Try not to bring the entire city back with you, General.”

“No promises.”

The transmission ended.

The command post returned to its usual background noise.

Kota turned toward the officers gathered around the central display.

“Listen up.”

The room gradually fell silent.

Several officers stopped what they were doing and faced him.

Kota pointed toward the tactical map.

“Planetary Defense will assume security of Oros.”

The communications officer immediately began preparing the transmission.

Kota continued.

“All Republic combat elements, prepare to return to the Iron Crag for reorganization and replenishment.”

The order was simple.

There was no ceremony in it.

No speech about victory.

They had secured the capital, broken the Separatist assault, and rescued the Senator. That was enough.

The communications officer looked up.

“General, should the order include the 108th, both infantry brigades, and all attached Republic units?”

“All combat elements,” Kota said. “Nobody stays behind unless they’re wounded or assigned to the planetary handover.”

“Understood.”

The officer turned toward the communications console.

“Preparing transmission.”

Across the command post, terminals came alive.

Encrypted channels opened one after another. The command network began linking with Republic units throughout the city, connecting the command post to forward positions, artillery crews, landing zones, and temporary medical stations.

Kota watched as the order moved through the system.

The first confirmation appeared.

1st Infantry Brigade — RECEIVED.

A second followed.

2nd Expeditionary Brigade — RECEIVED.

Then:

108th Clone Regiment — RECEIVED.

More confirmations began filling the display.

The officer read them aloud as they arrived.

“Artillery groups acknowledging.”

A pause.

“Forward medical units acknowledging.”

Another pause.

“Reconnaissance elements acknowledging.”

Kota looked toward the main tactical display.

Across Oros, blue markers began shifting.

Some units started withdrawing from defensive positions. Others began gathering at designated extraction points. Heavy vehicles were being prepared for transport, while clone troopers and militia soldiers received orders to return to their staging areas.

The battle lines were disappearing.

Not because the war was over.

Because, for the moment, Oros no longer needed them.

The communications officer looked up from his console.

“All major formations have acknowledged the order, General.”

Kota nodded.

“Send the final instruction.”

“What should it say?”

Kota looked at the ruined city beyond the command post’s viewport.

“Tell them to move carefully.”

The officer waited.

Kota’s expression hardened.

“And tell them to bring everyone they can.”

The officer nodded.

“Yes, General.”

The final transmission went out across the Republic command network.

To all Republic combat elements operating on Oros: planetary defense forces will assume responsibility for city security. All Republic units are to disengage from current operations and prepare for return to the Iron Crag. Report to assigned extraction points for reorganization, medical evaluation, and replenishment. Wounded personnel will be transferred under medical priority. Maintain defensive readiness until relieved.

For a moment, the command post listened to the message repeating through the network.

Then the confirmations continued.

One after another.

Across the city, Kota’s soldiers began leaving their positions.

The last day of the Republic was approaching.

But for now, Oros had survived.

  

## CHP3 Scene 2 — Xorad

Deep beneath the rugged terrain of Kalyph, far from the burning streets of Oros, General Xorad paced across the cold metallic floor of his hidden command base.

Around him, machines hummed.

Holodisplays flickered.

Droids moved between consoles, processing reports from a battlefield that no longer existed in their favor.

Xorad stopped.

The primary holotable displayed the city.

Red indicators representing Separatist forces were disappearing one after another.

Company.

Battalion.

Division.

Gone.

His calculations had failed.

For three hundred and forty-two days, Xorad had studied General Rahm Kota.

He had mapped his movements.

His response times.

His defensive patterns.

His supply routes.

His habits.

He had studied every battle report available to the Separatist Alliance and constructed thousands of possible outcomes.

Kota’s army should have broken.

Instead, it had done the opposite.

The unexpected intervention of an unidentified Jedi had disrupted Xorad’s calculations.

Kota’s militia had refused to collapse under pressure.

And his two thousand elite clones had fought with a level of cohesion that Xorad’s models had repeatedly classified as statistically unsustainable.

The assault on Oros had failed.

Completely.

Xorad stared at the holotable.

Then his head turned toward the command console.

“Realign all remaining planetary divisions.”

A T-series tactical droid approached.

“General Xorad, ninety-seven point two-one-one-five-three-eight-five percent of our entire corps has been destroyed.”

Xorad did not move.

“Only three full battalions remain operational.”

The tactical droid paused.

“A full commitment against the entrenched Republic position will result in the complete loss of our remaining forces. Victory is statistically impossible.”

Xorad finally looked at him.

“Then we shall alter the calculation.”

The tactical droid’s photoreceptors narrowed.

“General?”

“Prepare all remaining battle droids.”

“For what purpose?”

Xorad turned back toward the holotable.

“For an assault on the Iron Crag.”

There was a brief silence.

“General, the Iron Crag is heavily fortified. Its defensive capabilities exceed those of Oros. A direct assault would result in—”

“I know.”

“Then I must advise against—”

“Advice noted.”

Xorad returned to the console.

His metallic fingers moved across the controls.

The fortress appeared above the table.

Massive.

Layered.

Defended from every direction.

His processors ran another simulation.

The result appeared in red.

PROBABILITY OF SUCCESS: 0.73%

Xorad stared at the number.

Less than one percent.

The tactical droid stepped closer.

“General, this confirms my assessment.”

Xorad said nothing.

The simulation continued running.

He watched the numbers change.

0.71%.

0.69%.

0.74%.

Then stabilize.

Xorad’s head tilted slightly.

“Kota has demonstrated an anomaly.”

The tactical droid waited.

“He repeatedly achieves victory when conventional probability predicts defeat.”

“General, that is precisely why the previous operation failed.”

“No.”

Xorad looked at him.

“That is why the previous operation succeeded.”

The tactical droid remained silent.

Xorad continued.

“My models assumed that Kota would behave rationally when confronted with overwhelming force.”

He looked at the burning representation of Oros.

“He did not.”

The image changed.

Kota’s forces appeared across the battlefield.

“He relied upon loyalty.”

Another image.

Militia and clones fighting side by side.

“He relied upon individuals acting beyond their predicted limits.”

Another.

The unidentified Jedi.

“And he accepted variables that no algorithm could reliably measure.”

Xorad turned away from the holotable.

“Heart. Loyalty. Sacrifice. Defiance.”

His voice remained completely mechanical.

Yet something had changed.

“I classified these variables as weaknesses.”

He looked back toward the fortress.

“I was incorrect.”

The tactical droid processed this.

“Then what is your conclusion?”

Xorad stared at the less-than-one-percent probability.

“The illogical can overcome the inevitable.”

He reached toward the console.

“Therefore, we will become illogical.”

The tactical droid hesitated.

“General, you are proposing the deliberate destruction of the remaining sector forces.”

“Yes.”

“For a probability of less than one percent.”

“Yes.”

“That is irrational.”

Xorad’s photoreceptors brightened.

“Exactly.”

He activated the final command.

Across the subterranean base, dormant battle droids began powering up.

Thousands of metallic feet struck the floors.

Weapon systems activated.

Transport vessels began preparing for launch.

The tactical droid looked toward Xorad.

“If we proceed, General, there is a high probability that none of us will survive.”

Xorad watched the fortress disappear from the holotable.

“Then we will ensure that our failure is worthy of the Separatist Alliance.”

For the first time since the Battle of Oros began, General Xorad had stopped calculating how to survive.

He was calculating how to die.

## CHP3 Scene 3 — Return to the Iron Crag

  

The last transports returned to the Iron Crag beneath a sky still heavy with the smoke of the day's fighting.

One by one, LAATs descended into the fortress.

Their engines screamed through the hangar approaches before settling onto their landing platforms. Some touched down cleanly.

Others did not.

One gunship came in with scorch marks running across its hull and half of one stabilizer wing hanging loose.

  

Another had a section of armor plating missing entirely.

  

Mechanics were already waiting.

  

The moment the battered gunships settled flat against the deck, ground crews rushed forward.

Some climbed onto the hull before the engines had even fully powered down.

Others dragged equipment beneath the gunships, inspecting damaged repulsor pads and leaking coolant lines.

A militia mechanic looked up at one particularly battered LAAT.

He shook his head.

“Could’ve at least brought it back with the wings attached.”

The clone pilot climbed out of the cockpit and looked down at him.

“You wanted it returned.”

The mechanic pointed at the missing section of the hull.

“You didn’t think to mention the condition?”

The clone shrugged.

“You never said in one piece.”

The mechanic stared at him.

Then laughed.

“Get out of my hangar.”

The clone laughed as he walked away.

Around them, Iron Crag continued moving.

It had been waiting for them.

The fortress had its own rhythm, and the return of thousands of soldiers simply became another part of it.

Cargo crews moved ammunition away from incoming transports.

Medical teams directed wounded personnel toward the medical wing.

Technicians rolled power cells toward charging stations.

Officers hurried between command posts.

Somewhere overhead, warning lights flashed as another damaged gunship descended into the hangar.

The great fortress swallowed them all.

Kota walked.

He didn’t need to give orders.

Not yet.

The officers already knew what needed to be done.

He moved away from the hangars and into one of the fortress’s main recovery concourses.

It was a broad interior avenue connecting the hangar complex to the barracks, mess facilities, medical sections, and internal transport corridors.

Thousands of people passed through it every day.

Today, it felt different.

Alive.

A group of clone troopers sat around a long table outside one of the mess areas.

Their armor had been removed.

For once, they looked less like soldiers and more like exhausted young men.

They ate whatever had been put in front of them.

One clone was telling a story.

“And then the tank comes around the corner—”

A militia volunteer across from him leaned forward.

“How big?”

The clone held his hands apart.

“That big.”

“That wasn’t a tank.”

Another clone looked up from his meal.

“It was absolutely a tank.”

The first clone continued.

“I grab the detonator, jump onto the thing, and—”

A clone sitting farther down the table rolled his eyes.

“That’s not how it happened.”

The table erupted in laughter.

The storyteller pointed at him.

“You weren’t there.”

“I was literally standing next to you.”

“Exactly. You were too busy hiding.”

More laughter.

A bottle of some local Kalyph drink sat in the middle of the table.

Someone had already opened it.

Nobody seemed particularly concerned about where it had come from.

At another table, two militia volunteers argued over who had destroyed more battle droids.

A clone trooper listened to them for several seconds before quietly saying:

“Neither of you kept count.”

Both men looked at him.

The clone returned to eating.

Across the concourse, another group had gathered around a holoprojector.

Someone had pulled up a crude tactical reconstruction of the battle.

A soldier pointed at the display.

“That’s where Kel-Zor came through.”

“No, it isn’t.”

“It is.”

“That was three streets over.”

“Same street.”

“Not even close.”

Kota walked past them.

Nobody stopped him.

Nobody needed to.

For a few moments, he simply watched.

This was what the fortress looked like when the war wasn’t demanding everything from it.

People eating.

People arguing.

People laughing.

People telling stories that would become more ridiculous every time they were retold.

The sound traveled through the enormous interior.

For all its durasteel walls and military corridors, Iron Crag did not feel like a fortress in those moments.

It felt like a home.

Then Kota heard the sound of repulsor engines approaching from the opposite end of the concourse.

He turned.

A casualty transport emerged from a side corridor.

The atmosphere changed immediately.

Nobody announced it.

Nobody ordered anyone to be quiet.

They simply became quiet.

Medical personnel guided the transport toward the medical wing.

Behind it came several soldiers carrying stretchers.

Friends.

Comrades.

Men and women who had been fighting beside them only hours earlier.

The laughter from the mess tables faded.

Several clones stood.

A militia volunteer stepped aside and removed his helmet as the stretchers passed.

Nobody spoke.

One of the stretchers was covered.

Another wasn’t.

A clone walking beside it kept one hand resting against the edge of the stretcher as he moved.

At the end of the procession, a medical technician carried a small crate.

An empty clone helmet sat on top.

The technician stopped briefly to adjust his grip.

A clone trooper passing in the opposite direction noticed the helmet.

He stopped.

His eyes remained fixed on it.

Slowly, he reached out and touched the top of the helmet with two fingers.

Just for a moment.

Then he lowered his hand.

The technician continued toward the medical wing.

The clone watched him go.

Then he turned and kept walking.

Behind him, the mess hall slowly came back to life.

A laugh.

Then another.

A conversation resumed.

Someone dropped a utensil.

Someone complained about the food.

The fortress continued breathing.

Kota stood in the middle of it all.

He looked toward the mess hall.

Then toward the medical wing.

Both were part of the same fortress.

Both were part of the same war.

And all of it was what they were fighting to protect.

  

Ash finally found an empty seat in the mess hall.

For the first time since leaving Oros, he allowed himself to sit.

The mess hall was louder than the battlefield had been in some ways. Clones talked over one another from crowded tables. Militia volunteers traded stories across the room. Trays scraped against metal. Someone near the far wall had found a bottle of local liquor and was already trying to convince everyone within earshot that it was the finest drink in the galaxy.

Ash barely heard any of it.

He leaned back and looked around the table.

Something was missing.

He looked toward the entrance.

Then back at the table.

“Where’s Novek?”

One of the clone troopers sitting nearby looked up from his meal.

“He was with General Kel-Zor.”

Ash frowned.

“With Kel-Zor?”

The clone nodded.

“Last I saw him.”

Ash leaned back again.

“Well, that’s probably not good.”

A voice came from behind him.

“Why wouldn’t it be good?”

Ash turned.

Novek was walking toward the table with a grin on his face.

Ash stared at him.

“What are you so happy about?”

Novek stopped beside the table.

“I got promoted.”

The table went quiet for half a second.

Ash blinked.

“To what?”

“Corporal.”

A grin spread across Ash’s face.

“Who promoted you?”

“General Kel-Zor.”

The reaction was immediate.

The clones at the table slapped the table. One of the militia soldiers whistled. Another reached across and shook Novek’s hand.

“About time.”

“Don’t let the rank go to your head.”

“Corporal Volsign. Sounds strange.”

Novek laughed.

“Yeah, well, get used to it.”

Ash stood and grabbed Novek by the shoulder.

“Congratulations, Corporal.”

“Thank you, Sergeant.”

Ash narrowed his eyes.

“Don’t start.”

Novek laughed again and dropped into the empty seat beside him.

For a few moments, there was nothing else.

No battlefield.

No droids.

No artillery.

Just soldiers eating together.

Novek picked up his tray.

“After I finish this, I’m going to check on the Corporal Pivot.”

Ash looked at him.

“The one who got hit covering you?”

Novek nodded.

“Yeah.”

Ash looked around the table.

“We should visit the rest of our guys while we're down there. The whole ward.”

  

Nobody objected.

  

One of the clones nodded.

  

“Agreed. No one in Trench Squad sits in med-bay alone.”

  

“Yeah.”

  

“Definitely.”

  

Novek looked around at them, then down at his food.

“I guess I owe him now.”

Ash smiled.

“He isn’t the first.”

Novek looked at him.

“Unfortunately.”

The table erupted in laughter.

Ash laughed with them.

For the first time since Oros, it felt easy.

  

Elsewhere in the fortress, Commander Vantage moved through the barracks.

He did not sit down.

Not yet.

He walked among his troops.

He stopped beside one table and spoke with a pair of clones who had returned with the first wave. He checked their equipment, asked about their ammunition, and listened while one of them described how a Separatist tank had nearly flattened their position.

At another table, he congratulated a lieutenant on holding his position under heavy fire.

“Good work.”

The lieutenant straightened.

“Thank you, Commander.”

Vantage continued down the row.

He stopped beside another group.

One clone was missing part of his armor. Another had a bandage wrapped around his arm.

“You should be in medical.”

“I’ve already been cleared, sir.”

Vantage studied him for a moment.

“Then get some food.”

“Yes, sir.”

Vantage moved on.

Near the medical corridor, he stopped to speak with wounded troopers as they were being treated. He knew some of their names. Others he knew by designation.

He asked about their injuries.

He asked whether they needed anything.

He listened.

Then he continued.

At the end of the corridor, a young clone officer approached him.

The officer hesitated.

“Commander.”

Vantage stopped.

“Sir… I wanted to apologize.”

“For what?”

The officer swallowed.

“We lost men.”

Vantage looked at him.

The officer expected anger.

There was none.

Vantage simply said,

“They completed the mission.”

The officer looked down.

Vantage placed a hand on his shoulder.

“Make sure you do the same next time.”

“Yes, sir.”

Vantage walked away.

There was no speech.

No ceremony.

Just a commander making sure his soldiers knew that their sacrifices had mattered.

  

Kota saw all of it.

He did not interrupt.

He walked through the fortress and watched.

Ash and Novek laughing with the rest of Trench Squad.

Clones eating beside militia volunteers.

Wounded soldiers being carried toward medical care.

Vantage moving among his men.

Officers being congratulated.

Soldiers simply sitting together after surviving another battle.

For a few minutes, Iron Crag did not feel like a military fortress.

It felt like home.

Kota stopped at the edge of the mess hall.

He watched Ash say something that made Novek laugh again.

A barely visible smile crossed Kota’s face.

For one brief afternoon, his army felt like a family.

Kota turned away.

He walked down the corridor alone.

Behind him, the mess hall remained alive with voices and laughter.

For now, nobody knew what was coming.

And for now, that was enough.

## CHP3 Scene 4 — The Private Conversation

Kota’s office was one of the few places in the Iron Crag where the noise of the fortress seemed to disappear.

The room was modest by the standards of the massive installation. A tactical table occupied most of the center, surrounded by scattered datapads, maps, and reports. A weapons cabinet stood against one wall. Another held a small collection of objects Kota had accumulated over the years—pieces of campaigns, reminders of people and places he refused to forget.

Kota stood behind his desk, reading a casualty report.

The door opened.

One of his elite militia guards stepped inside.

“General.”

Kota looked up.

“Rogue Zero is here to see you.”

Kota set the datapad down.

“Send him in.”

The guard stepped aside.

Rogue Zero entered.

Another elite guard followed him and stopped just inside the room.

Kota looked toward both guards.

“Leave us.”

The two soldiers immediately turned and stepped outside.

The door sealed behind them.

Silence returned.

For several seconds, neither Jedi spoke.

Ronen finally broke it.

“You always in here?”

Kota nodded.

“Best place to think.”

Ronen glanced around the room.

“Not much has changed.”

“Didn’t need to.”

Ronen looked back at him.

“You look older.”

Kota raised an eyebrow.

“A wise Jedi once told me war does that.”

Ronen smiled.

“Sounds like something you’d say.”

“It was.”

A quiet laugh passed between them.

Kota looked at the scar running across the right side of Ronen’s face.

“You’ve still got that scar.”

Ronen touched the corner of his mouth.

“Occupational hazard.”

Kota chuckled.

It was brief.

Almost imperceptible.

But it was there.

For a moment, neither was a Jedi General nor a secret operative.

They were simply master and apprentice who had not seen each other in far too long.

Kota gestured toward the chair.

Ronen sat.

Kota remained standing.

“How’s the Network?”

Ronen’s expression changed slightly.

“Growing.”

“Reliable?”

“More every month.”

Kota nodded.

He already knew the answer mattered.

Ronen leaned back.

“We’ve got contacts on worlds we never had access to before. Traders. Pilots. Independent suppliers. A few people inside places they shouldn’t be.”

“Good.”

“It’s working.”

Kota folded his arms.

“How far?”

“Far enough that I don’t think the Republic even knows how much we’ve built.”

“That was the point.”

Ronen nodded.

Neither of them spoke for several seconds.

Then Ronen’s expression became more serious.

“The contact finally reached out.”

Kota’s attention sharpened.

“What did they say?”

Ronen looked toward the floor for a moment, choosing his words carefully.

“They said they’ve found a pocket.”

“A pocket?”

“A pocket of the galaxy. Cut off from the war.”

Kota waited.

“A safe haven?”

“Potentially.”

Ronen’s voice lowered.

“But it’s not a paradise.”

Kota said nothing.

“The contact says a major conflict is already raging inside this pocket.”

“What kind of conflict?”

“A war.”

“How large?”

Ronen looked back at him.

“Billions of soldiers.”

Kota’s expression hardened.

“Billions?”

“That’s what the contact reported.”

“And they’re completely isolated?”

“Completely.”

“From the Republic?”

“Yes.”

“The Separatists?”

“Yes.”

Kota studied him.

“No clone factories?”

“None.”

“No battle droids?”

“None that the contact has seen.”

Ronen paused.

“From what I’ve been told, they’re free from our war.”

Kota looked toward the small viewport overlooking the interior of the fortress.

Below them, personnel moved through the corridors.

Clones.

Militia.

Mechanics.

Medics.

People who had spent years fighting a war that seemed to have no end.

Ronen continued.

“Free from the specific hatred that’s swallowing our galaxy.”

Kota remained silent.

Ronen stood.

“Master…”

Kota looked back at him.

“We both know something terrible is coming.”

Kota’s expression did not change.

“We’ve both felt it.”

Neither Jedi spoke for a moment.

The Force had shown them fragments.

Warnings without explanations.

Darkness approaching from somewhere neither of them could see.

They had never been able to understand what it meant.

Only that something was coming.

Ronen stepped closer.

“We don’t know what it is. We don’t know when it happens.”

His voice became quieter.

“But if the Republic falls…”

He paused.

“Or if the Separatists win…”

Another pause.

“We need somewhere beyond their reach.”

Kota looked at him.

Ronen’s eyes held no fear.

Only conviction.

“This pocket may be the only real contingency we have.”

Kota was silent.

Then he asked the question that mattered.

“Do you believe the contact?”

Ronen didn’t hesitate.

“I do.”

“Why?”

Ronen looked down briefly.

Then back at his master.

“Because after everything I’ve seen…”

He took a breath.

“And everything we’ve foreseen…”

His voice was quiet.

“I have to believe something like this can exist.”

Kota studied him for a long moment.

Neither man spoke.

Outside the office, the Iron Crag continued around them.

Boots struck metal floors.

Ships were being repaired.

Orders were being issued.

Soldiers were eating, laughing, treating wounds, preparing for the next battle.

The war had not stopped.

It was still waiting outside.

Kota finally straightened.

“Then we keep the information between us.”

Ronen nodded.

“For now.”

Kota reached for the datapad on his desk.

“Let’s get back to work.”

Ronen smiled faintly.

“Still no time to rest?”

“Never was.”

They turned toward the door.

Kota opened it.

The two elite guards immediately straightened.

Ronen stepped into the corridor beside his master.

For a brief moment, the two Jedi walked together through the heart of the fortress.

Behind them, hidden in the Unknown Regions, a possible refuge waited beyond the reach of the war.

Ahead of them, the Republic was still fighting.

And neither man knew that the darkness they had sensed was already much closer than they imagined.

## CHP3 Scene 5 — The Command Center After

The command center had settled into a strange kind of calm.

The battle was over.

For the first time in hours, no emergency alarms were sounding. Officers moved between consoles with reports in hand, updating maps and reorganizing units. Communications officers spoke quietly into headsets while technicians worked through the mountain of information coming in from Oros.

General Jarek Turnmaash stood near the central tactical display with Brigadier Generals Jaxen Kel-Zor and Toren Trowk.

Turnmaash folded his arms.

“Did you boys hear?”

Trowk glanced at him.

“Hear what?”

Turnmaash grinned.

“Apparently General Kenobi has engaged General Grievous on Utapau.”

Kel-Zor raised an eyebrow.

“Really?”

Turnmaash nodded.

“Imagine that. First Count Dooku, now General Grievous.”

Kel-Zor shook his head.

“May be one bad week for the Separatists.”

“Indeed.”

Turnmaash looked toward the tactical display.

“Who knows? Maybe we’ll see the end of the war this week.”

“I wouldn’t count on it.”

The three generals turned.

Kota walked through the entrance to the command center.

Rogue Zero followed beside him, his hood lowered, while six elite militia guards entered behind them.

Two more guards remained outside the command center, positioned on either side of the entrance.

Turnmaash smiled.

“Glad to see you back in one piece.”

Kota gave him a brief look.

“I wouldn’t trust any news coming from Coruscant.”

Kel-Zor gave a short laugh.

“That’s the spirit.”

Kota walked toward the central tactical display.

The room gradually returned to business.

Tala Vess’undri stepped forward with a datapad in hand.

Kota looked at her.

“Status on the Separatist forces?”

Tala activated the display.

“The latest intelligence indicates that ninety-seven percent of General Xorad’s corps has been destroyed.”

Several officers looked up.

Kota studied the numbers.

“A corps.”

“Yes, General.”

Tala expanded the tactical map.

“Xorad committed the majority of his planetary forces to Oros. What we encountered wasn’t a reinforced division or a localized assault.”

She pointed toward the display.

“It was a full Separatist corps.”

Kota looked across the room.

“And the remaining three percent?”

“Retreated.”

“Where?”

“We don’t know.”

## CHP3 Scene 10 — The News of The Chip

By the time Kota arrived, the hangar was already filling.

The enormous facility had been cleared of its usual operations, with ground equipment pushed toward the outer walls and maintenance platforms repositioned to make room. Temporary lighting illuminated the central floor, while several rows of medical stations had been established along the hangar’s perimeter. Medical droids moved between them, running diagnostic checks and preparing surgical equipment.

The preparations were underway.

Protocol Omega Eleven had been activated.

Militia personnel had also been mobilized throughout Iron Crag. Unlike their previous assignments, the main militia brigades had now received authorization to carry weapons. Armed personnel occupied the hangar entrances, access corridors, and surrounding security positions. Others were already assisting the medical staff with equipment, supplies, and the movement of patients.

They would be essential in the hours ahead.

The clone personnel were arriving from every section of the fortress.

Members of the deployed regiment stood alongside Venator crewmen, Acclamator personnel, pilots, technicians, and support specialists. Officers had organized their respective units into manageable formations, although the usual military order was noticeably less precise.

There were too many unanswered questions.

Before the battle, Iron Crag’s clone complement had numbered approximately eleven thousand three hundred to eleven thousand five hundred personnel. Not all of them were present now.

Three hundred and four clones from the deployed regiment had been killed or wounded during the fighting. Among those with critical injuries, the estimated risk of death remained considerable. Others were still recovering from their injuries or performing duties that could not immediately be abandoned.

The medical facilities were already treating as many as they could accommodate.

Nevertheless, thousands had assembled.

At the far end of the hangar, Kota stood beside a raised command platform. Kel-Zor was nearby, speaking quietly with one of the militia officers. Ronen stood a short distance behind them, watching the gathering.

Bor Regg had arrived as well, although he remained near the medical stations rather than joining the officers on the platform. He had insisted that someone from his medical team be available to answer technical questions.

Then Kota noticed Vantage approaching.

The clone commander was still recovering. His movements were somewhat slower than usual, and a medical technician had accompanied him to ensure he didn’t overexert himself.

Kota looked at him.

“You should be in bed.”

“I’ve spent enough time there, General.”

“Stay close. Don’t push yourself.”

“Yes, sir.”

Kota turned toward the gathered personnel.

“Let’s get started.”

A communications officer stepped forward and activated the hangar’s internal announcement system.

“All personnel, attention. General Kota will now address the assembled garrison.”

Conversations gradually faded.

“At ease.”
