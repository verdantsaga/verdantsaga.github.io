# Chapter Two: Battle of Oros

## CHP2 Scene 1 – The Command Center

The doors opened.

Kota walked into the command center.

The room had transformed since he had left it.

Officers crowded around the tactical displays. Communications flooded every station. Holographic maps shifted constantly as new reports came in, disappearing almost as quickly as they appeared.

“Enemy contacts confirmed over Oros!”

“North defense grid is offline!”

“Another planetary battery has been destroyed!”

“Evacuation transports are being redirected!”

“We’ve lost contact with Patrol Group Aurek-Seven!”

Kota continued walking.

“General!”

He stopped.

Commander Vantage approached him.

“Report.”

“Planetary defense forces are engaged across the capital. Separatist armor has broken through the northern defensive line and landed troops in three districts.”

Kota looked toward the main display.

The holographic image of Oros rotated above the tactical table.

Red indicators covered the city.

“How many?”

“Unknown.”

“Unknown isn’t a number.”

Vantage didn’t flinch.

“We’ve lost too many sensors to give you an accurate count.”

Kota looked at the display.

“Give me your best estimate.”

“At least two divisions.”

“Separatist?”

“Yes.”

“Armor?”

“Heavy.”

“Air support?”

“Confirmed.”

Kota’s expression hardened.

“Then this isn’t a raid.”

“No, General.”

Tala stepped forward.

“It’s a coordinated assault.”

Kota looked at her.

“Explain.”

She brought up several layers of intelligence data.

“The attacks we’ve been tracking weren’t independent.”

The previous interference appeared on the display.

“They were preparing the battlefield.”

Kota studied it.

“The communications disruption.”

“Part of it.”

“The patrol attacks.”

“Testing our response.”

“The weapons shipments.”

“Positioning.”

Tala looked toward Oros.

“And now they’re committing everything.”

A communications officer called from across the room.

“General!”

Kota turned.

“We’ve recovered a transmission from one of the patrol groups.”

“Play it.”

Static filled the command center.

A broken voice came through.

“—taking fire—”

Static.

“—too many—”

A burst of interference.

“—we’re surrounded—”

Then silence.

The transmission ended.

Nobody spoke.

Kota looked at the communications officer.

“Which patrol?”

“Aurek-Seven.”

“Status?”

The officer hesitated.

“No survivors confirmed.”

Kota’s jaw tightened.

“Another.”

The officer pulled up another report.

“Patrol Group Besh-Four.”

“What happened?”

“Overrun.”

“Survivors?”

“None confirmed.”

Kota looked toward Tala.

“How many patrols?”

She checked her display.

“Four that we know of.”

“All overrun?”

“Yes.”

Kota looked back at the hologram.

Four small patrol groups.

Men who had been outside the capital when the attack began.

They had been the first to encounter the Separatists.

And the Separatists had wiped them out.

Kota’s voice became quieter.

“Mark them.”

Tala looked at him.

“General?”

“Every one.”

She understood.

“Yes.”

Their locations appeared on the map.

Four markers.

Four patrols.

Four losses.

Kota stared at them for a moment.

Then he looked toward the main display.

“Planetary defense forces?”

Vantage answered.

“Still fighting.”

“How long?”

“Not long enough.”

Kota looked at him.

“Meaning?”

“They’re being pushed back.”

Another transmission came through.

“Planetary defense command reports the western district has fallen.”

Another officer shouted.

“Governor’s office is requesting immediate military support!”

Kota turned toward the communications station.

“Tell the governor we’re coming.”

The officer hesitated.

“Sir, he wants to know how many forces we’re sending.”

Kota stared at him.

“All of them.”

The officer blinked.

“General?”

“You heard me.”

“Yes, General.”

The officer turned back to his station.

Kota looked around the room.

The decision had been made.

Now everyone simply had to make it happen.

“Vantage.”

The clone commander stepped forward.

“General.”

“How quickly can you get the regiment ready?”

“Immediately.”

“How many combat companies?”

“All of them.”

Kota nodded.

“Get them aboard.”

Vantage turned away.

Then stopped.

“General.”

Kota looked at him.

“We’ll be leaving the fortress lightly defended.”

Kota’s eyes hardened.

“We won’t be here long enough for that to matter.”

Vantage nodded.

“Understood.”

He moved away.

Kota turned toward Tally.

“Transports.”

Tally was already working.

“Twenty-two available immediately.”

“Not enough.”

“No.”

“How many can you get?”

Tally hesitated.

“Thirty-eight if we pull every reserve transport from the southern hangars.”

“Do it.”

“That will disrupt supply operations.”

Kota looked at him.

“Then supply can wait.”

Tally nodded.

“Yes, General.”

Grissom approached.

“General.”

Kota looked at him.

“You’re going to hate this.”

“Try me.”

“Thirty-eight transports means we’re taking a substantial amount of fuel and ammunition with us.”

“How much?”

“Enough that the Iron Crag will have to run on reduced reserves.”

Kota stared at him.

“For how long?”

“Depends how long the battle lasts.”

“Then we’ll finish quickly.”

Grissom sighed.

“I was afraid you’d say that.”

Kota turned toward Jarek.

The militia general stood at the opposite side of the table, staring at the burning image of Oros.

“Jarek.”

He looked up.

“Yeah?”

“You’re staying.”

Jarek’s expression immediately hardened.

“No.”

Kota stared at him.

“That wasn’t a question.”

“My people are going.”

“Yes.”

“Then I’m going.”

“No.”

Jarek stepped toward him.

“Kota—”

“You command the 108th from here.”

“I can command them from Oros.”

“You can command them better from here.”

Jarek shook his head.

“You’ve always had a problem with leaving people behind.”

Kota’s eyes narrowed.

“Don’t confuse command with cowardice.”

Jarek went quiet.

Kota continued.

“If something happens to the Iron Crag while we’re gone, I need someone here who knows these people.”

Jarek looked around the room.

The officers.

The militia personnel.

The clone commanders.

The fortress beyond the walls.

Kota stepped closer.

“That’s you.”

Jarek stared at him.

“And you?”

“I’m going to Oros.”

“Of course you are.”

Kota didn’t answer.

Jarek looked back at the hologram.

The capital was still burning.

Finally, he nodded.

“Fine.”

Kota turned away.

Jarek called after him.

“Kota.”

He stopped.

“Bring my people back.”

Kota looked over his shoulder.

“I’ll bring back whoever’s still alive.”

Jarek’s expression tightened.

It was not comforting.

It was honest.

And somehow that made it worse.

Jarek nodded.

“That’s good enough.”

Kota turned toward the tactical table.

“Tala.”

She looked up.

“Find me their command structure.”

“I’m already working on it.”

“Good.”

“Trowk.”

The quiet general stepped forward.

“General.”

“Prepare the Second Expeditionary Brigade.”

“Full deployment?”

“Yes.”

Trowk nodded.

“Understood.”

“Kel-Zor.”

The enormous cyborg approached.

A faint grin crossed his face.

“Finally.”

Kota looked at him.

“First Brigade.”

Kel-Zor’s grin disappeared.

“Ready.”

“Get them moving.”

“Gladly.”

He turned and left.

Kota looked toward Vantage.

“Status?”

The clone commander checked his datapad.

“First companies are boarding.”

“How long?”

“Ten minutes.”

“Make it five.”

Vantage looked at him.

“Five.”

Kota stared back.

“Do it.”

Vantage nodded.

“Yes, General.”

He left.

The command center continued moving around Kota.

Orders were transmitted.

Ships were prepared.

Troops were mobilized.

Vehicles were fueled.

A war that had existed on reports and tactical displays for years had suddenly become real.

Oros was no longer a distant problem.

It was a battlefield.

And the Iron Crag was about to empty itself into it.

Kota looked once more at the hologram.

The burning capital filled his vision.

He turned toward the doors.

Jarek watched him.

“You really think we can stop this?”

Kota stopped.

He didn’t look back.

“I don’t think.”

Jarek waited.

Kota finally turned his head.

“I know.”

The doors opened.

Kota walked out.

Behind him, the command center erupted into motion.

Jarek remained at the tactical table.

Vantage led the clones toward the hangars.

Kel-Zor moved to rally the First Brigade.

Trowk began preparing the expeditionary forces.

Tally coordinated transports.

Grissom calculated supplies.

Tala hunted for the Separatist command structure.

And across the Iron Crag, thousands of soldiers prepared for battle.

None of them had fought yet.

That was about to change.

  

## CHP2 Scene 2 – Scramble

“By order of General Kota: all units of the Iron Crag are to begin immediate combat deployment.”

The announcement echoed through every level of the fortress.

“First Infantry Brigade, 108th Republic Liberty Corps, is ordered to immediate deployment to Oros.”

“Second Expeditionary Brigade is ordered to immediate deployment readiness.”

“Clone Regiment is ordered to immediate deployment readiness.”

“All available aerospace units are to provide immediate support to the planetary defense forces at Oros.”

“All commanding officers are to report to their assigned staging areas.”

The announcement ended.

For half a second, nothing happened.

Then the Iron Crag came alive.

Lights illuminated across the massive hangars.

Blast doors opened.

Engines roared to life.

Thousands of soldiers began moving through the fortress.

There was no confusion.

No panic.

No hesitation.

The orders had been given.

Everyone knew their place.

  

The first movement came from the 1st Infantry Brigade.

Brigadier General Jaxen Kel-Zor stood on the hangar floor as the first wave of militia soldiers rushed toward the waiting LAATs.

“Move!”

His voice carried across the hangar.

“First company, get aboard!”

Militia soldiers ran beneath the enormous hangar lights.

Unlike the clone formations elsewhere in the fortress, these men and women were volunteers.

They wore the equipment of soldiers who had chosen to fight.

Some carried Republic-standard rifles.

Others carried weapons acquired from years of fighting alongside the Republic.

All of them were moving toward the same destination.

Oros.

The first LAAT/i’s engines were already turning.

A ground crewman raised a hand.

“First transport clear!”

Kel-Zor looked toward the open side doors.

“How long?”

“Two minutes!”

“You have one.”

The crewman immediately turned.

“One minute! Move!”

The militia soldiers accelerated.

A squad rushed through the wide side doors.

Another followed closely behind.

Another followed.

Inside the gunship, there were no comfortable seats waiting for them.

The soldiers stood along the sides of the troop bay, gripping the overhead straps as the engines vibrated beneath their boots.

A squad leader checked his men.

“Everybody secure!”

“Secure!”

“Check weapons!”

Rifles were checked.

“Check packs!”

Straps were tightened.

“Check each other!”

The soldiers looked to the men and women beside them.

“Ready!”

The first LAAT/i’s heavy side doors began to slide shut, sealing the troops inside.

Kel-Zor watched it from the deck.

“Launch.”

The gunship lifted.

Its engines thundered against the hangar walls.

The LAAT rose above the deck, turned toward the open launch corridor, and accelerated into the night.

Outside the fortress, the sky was black.

Only the stars and the distant glow of Oros broke the darkness.

The first gunship disappeared into the night.

Then another followed.

Then another.

The first wave of the 1st Infantry Brigade was underway.

Kel-Zor watched the last of the first group vanish into the darkness.

He turned toward the next formation.

“Second wave!”

The staging area immediately began moving again.

  

Across the fortress, the 2nd Expeditionary Brigade was preparing for its own deployment.

Brigadier General Toren Trowk stood beside a tactical display, watching his militia forces assemble.

His soldiers moved at a deliberate pace.

There was urgency.

But there was no wasted movement.

“First battalion?”

“Ready.”

“Engineering teams?”

“Ready.”

“Heavy weapons?”

“Loading now.”

Trowk checked the time.

“How long?”

“Eight minutes.”

“Six.”

The officer nodded.

“Yes, General.”

Trowk turned toward another staging area.

The second brigade was equipped differently from Kel-Zor’s infantry.

Engineering equipment was being loaded.

Portable shield generators.

Field communications equipment.

Heavy ammunition.

Construction equipment.

Medical supplies.

Everything required to establish a fighting position after the brigade reached Oros.

A militia captain approached.

“General.”

Trowk looked toward him.

“Report.”

“We’re short two power-cell crates.”

“Why?”

“One was damaged during loading.”

“Replace it.”

“Already being done.”

“Good.”

The captain turned to leave.

Trowk stopped him.

“Check the replacements before they go aboard.”

The captain nodded.

“Yes, sir.”

Trowk returned his attention to the staging area.

The first wave of the 2nd Expeditionary Brigade wasn’t leaving yet.

Not tonight.

Not until the order came.

But every soldier was preparing as though it could come at any moment.

  

The clone barracks were moving just as quickly.

The Clone Regiment had received its orders.

They were not launching yet.

But they were preparing.

Armor was being secured.

Rifles were being checked.

Ammunition was being distributed.

Clone troopers moved through the armory in organized columns.

Their movements were almost mechanical.

But there was nothing mechanical about the speed.

They knew what was coming.

A clone sergeant walked along the line.

“Helmet seals.”

The troopers checked them.

“Weapon systems.”

Rifles were raised and inspected.

“Power cells.”

Magazines were checked.

“Medical equipment.”

Medpacks were secured.

The sergeant nodded.

“Move.”

The clones moved.

No shouting was necessary.

They had trained for this.

They had done this before.

The difference was that this time they weren’t preparing for another deployment order that might never come.

Oros was already burning.

Commander Vantage moved through the staging area, checking each company.

A clone lieutenant approached him.

“Commander.”

Vantage turned.

“Report.”

“All companies are assembling.”

“Transport?”

“Being assigned.”

“Heavy weapons?”

“Ready.”

Vantage nodded.

“Keep them ready.”

The lieutenant hesitated.

“Sir?”

“We haven’t received the launch order yet.”

“Understood.”

Vantage looked toward the hangar.

“Make sure they’re ready when we do.”

The lieutenant saluted.

“Yes, Commander.”

Vantage continued through the staging area.

The clones waited.

  

The vehicle hangars were even louder.

Mechanics rushed between vehicles as Republic walkers and transports were prepared for deployment.

An AT-TE stood in one of the maintenance bays.

Its armor was open.

Three mechanics worked beneath it.

“Power coupling!”

A mechanic threw the component across the bay.

Another caught it.

“Got it!”

“How long?”

“Two minutes!”

“You’ve got one!”

The mechanic looked toward him.

“Then stop talking!”

Another AT-TE began moving.

Its six massive legs struck the durasteel floor.

The walker had already been crewed.

Its crew climbed into position as it moved toward the staging area.

Above it, a LAAT/c waited.

Its massive lifting apparatus hung beneath the carrier.

The AT-TE moved beneath it.

A ground crewman raised both arms.

“Hold!”

The walker stopped.

“Forward two meters!”

The driver moved it.

“Stop!”

The LAAT/c lowered.

The lifting mechanism descended.

“Connection!”

The clamps locked onto the walker.

“Secure!”

The ground crewman checked the indicators.

“Green!”

He stepped backward.

“Clear!”

The LAAT/c engines increased in power.

The AT-TE remained suspended beneath it.

But the carrier did not launch.

Not yet.

The ground crew moved on to the next vehicle.

Another AT-TE was already approaching.

The process began again.

  

Above the vehicle decks, the fighter hangars were preparing for a different kind of battle.

ARC-170 starfighters sat beneath the hangar lights.

Their engines were already warming.

Pilots hurried toward them.

Ground crews disconnected fuel lines.

“Port stabilizer?”

“Green.”

“Repulsors?”

“Green.”

“Weapons?”

“Loaded.”

“Fuel?”

“Full.”

“Then get it out.”

The first ARC-170 rolled toward the launch position.

Its engines screamed.

The hangar doors opened.

Cold night air rushed into the fortress.

The fighter accelerated.

Then vanished into the darkness.

Another followed.

Then another.

Republic starfighters climbed into the night sky and turned toward Oros.

They were not waiting for the army.

The planetary defense forces were already fighting.

They needed air support immediately.

A flight controller watched the fighters disappear from the sensors.

“First flight airborne.”

“Destination?”

“Oros.”

“Transmit defense-force frequencies.”

“Already done.”

The controller looked toward the next fighter.

“Send them.”

Another ARC-170 launched.

  

On a lower level of the fortress, Sergeant Ash was preparing with his squad.

Trench Squad.

Seven soldiers stood around him.

Four clones.

Three volunteers.

The only squad in the Iron Crag organized that way.

Ash pulled his helmet over his head and locked it into place.

The world became quieter.

His helmet systems activated.

The squad’s identification markers appeared across his visor.

Seven names.

Seven positions.

Seven soldiers.

Ash checked his rifle.

Power cell.

Loaded.

He checked his ammunition.

Full.

His helmet turned toward the others.

“Trench Squad.”

Seven heads turned toward him.

“LAAT Thirteen.”

A clone trooper checked the staging display.

“That’s our transport.”

“Good.”

Ash turned toward the loading area.

“Let’s move.”

The squad began moving.

They passed rows of soldiers preparing for deployment.

Some were clones.

Some were militia.

All were moving toward the same war.

Ash kept pace with his squad.

He didn’t say anything else.

He didn’t need to.

  

Novek was already at LAAT Thirteen.

His helmet was secured.

He was helping the loadmaster move supply crates into the troop bay.

“That one goes against the bulkhead.”

Novek lifted the crate.

“Medical?”

“Yes.”

“Then it stays accessible.”

He moved it into place.

Another crate came down.

“Ammunition.”

Novek took it.

“Where?”

“Starboard side.”

He carried it aboard.

The LAAT’s engines rumbled around him.

The troop bay was beginning to fill.

But Trench Squad wasn’t boarding yet.

They had another job.

Novek looked toward the next pallet.

“What’s this?”

“Power cells.”

Novek checked the markings.

“Those don’t go on this transport.”

The loadmaster looked at him.

“Why not?”

“Wrong destination.”

The loadmaster checked his datapad.

There was a pause.

“You’re right.”

Novek pointed toward another stack.

“Those do.”

The loadmaster nodded.

“Good catch.”

Novek moved toward the correct crates.

Ash’s voice came over his helmet comm.

“Novek.”

Novek turned.

Ash and the rest of Trench Squad were approaching.

“Ready?”

Novek secured the last crate.

“Almost.”

Ash looked at the supply bay.

“You’ve been saying that for five minutes.”

“Because you keep distracting me.”

Ash looked toward the loadmaster.

“Are we good?”

The loadmaster checked his list.

“Supplies are secure.”

Ash nodded.

“Then let’s go.”

The squad moved toward LAAT Thirteen.

Behind them, the Iron Crag continued to roar.

The first wave of the 1st Infantry Brigade had already disappeared into the night.

The second wave was preparing.

The 2nd Expeditionary Brigade waited for its turn.

The Clone Regiment stood ready in its staging areas.

AT-TEs were being positioned.

Fighters were already on their way to Oros.

Mechanics were still working.

Supplies were still moving.

Nothing stopped.

The fortress had become a machine.

Every person inside it had a purpose.

Every vehicle had a destination.

Every unit knew where it belonged.

And somewhere beyond the walls, Oros was still burning.

Ash reached LAAT Thirteen.

He stepped through the wide side doors.

Novek followed.

Neither of them knew when they would leave.

But they knew one thing.

When the order came, Trench Squad would be ready.

The stepped through the wide side doors.

The engines continued to hum.

And the night waited.

  

## CHP2 Scene 3 – Flight to Oros

The hangar doors had been open for some time.

Night air swept through the massive flight deck as another LAAT lifted away from the Iron Crag.

Its engines roared against the darkness before the gunship climbed above the fortress and disappeared toward Oros.

Another followed.

Then another.

General Rahm Kota walked toward his waiting transport.

Commander Vantage walked beside him.

Clone Major Tally followed several paces behind, datapad in hand.

The first waves of the 108th had already departed.

Kota’s group was next.

Nobody spoke.

There was nothing to say.

The battle had already begun.

They reached the LAAT, its sliding side doors thrown wide open. 

Inside, a mix of clone troopers and militia soldiers stood packed along the troop bay, gripping the overhead straps as the repulsorlift engines vibrated beneath their boots.

Up in the cockpit, Rainer was already preparing for takeoff. 

Designated CT-5119, the clone pilot had been flying Kota for years. 

His helmet rested on the console beside his seat while he worked through the final pre-flight checks.

Kota walked alongside the gunship, heading toward the nose. 

He looked up to see that the transparisteel canopy was still propped open. There sat Rainer.

“Rainer.”

The clone leaned over the high edge of the cockpit frame, looking down to see the Jedi. “General.”

Kota looked straight up at the elevated pilot. 

“Status.”

“Ready to fly, General.”

“Weapons?”

“Ready, General.”

“Engines?”

“Ready, General.”

Kota nodded.

Rainer turned back toward his controls. 

“Then we’re wasting time, General.”

Kota gave him a dry, lingering look.

Rainer’s mouth twitched into a faint smirk. 

“Respectfully, General.”

Turning on his heel, Kota walked into the troop bay.

Vantage and Tally followed him.

The LAAT lifted.

The Iron Crag dropped beneath them.

For a few moments, the only sound was the steady roar of the engines.

Kota stood near the front of the troop bay.

Vantage stood nearby.

Neither spoke.

Below them, the fortress became smaller.

The massive walls.

The hangars.

The towers.

The endless lights.

Then the Iron Crag disappeared behind them.

Ahead, the horizon was beginning to brighten.

The sun had not yet risen.

But the darkness was already beginning to retreat.

Kota stared forward.

Then he saw it.

Smoke.

It stretched across the horizon in dark columns.

More appeared as the LAAT continued forward.

Vantage noticed it too.

“Oros.”

Kota nodded.

“Yes.”

Rainer’s voice came through the internal communications system.

“General, we’re clear of the Iron Crag.”

Kota looked through the open side door.

“Understood.”

The wind tore past the opening.

Far ahead, a faint orange glow appeared against the horizon.

Kota watched it.

The glow grew brighter.

Then another appeared.

Then another.

Fires.

Oros.

Kota’s expression hardened.

The city was burning.

Even from this distance, columns of smoke rose into the night.

Rainer’s voice returned over the internal comm.

“General, we’re approaching the outer combat zone.”

Kota kept his eyes on the horizon.

“How bad?”

There was a short pause.

“General, bad enough that I wouldn’t recommend sightseeing.”

Kota gave him a flat look toward the cockpit, though Rainer couldn’t see it.

“Just fly.”

“Yes, General.”

Kota raised his wrist comlink.

He activated the channel to the Iron Crag.

“Turnmaash.”

Static crackled for a moment.

Then Jarek Turnmaash answered.

“Kota.”

His voice was calm.

There was no panic behind it.

That was typical of Turnmaash.

He had fought beside Kota for years. He had watched the Jedi take forces that should have been destroyed and somehow turn them into victories.

If Kota was going to Oros, Turnmaash expected him to do what he always did.

Win.

“Give me the situation,” Kota said.

Turnmaash answered immediately.

“LZ-Aurek is active. Civilian evacuation is underway. We’ve got a small 1st Infantry Brigade contingent securing the landing zone, while civilian transports and militia transports are moving people out.”

Kota nodded.

“And Besh?”

“Kel-Zor is pushing hard.”

That sounded exactly like Kel-Zor.

“How far?”

“Far enough to make the Separatists uncomfortable,” Turnmaash said. “Not far enough to break their line.”

Kota looked toward the burning horizon.

“And Cresh?”

“Trowk has established his line. He’s taking heavy resistance, but he’s holding it.”

“And the artillery?”

“Setting up outside the city. Once they’re fully operational, they’ll have a clean view of the approaches.”

Kota lowered his wrist slightly.

The battlefield was beginning to take shape in his mind.

Aurek was evacuation.

Cresh was containment.

Besh was the pressure point.

“What about the 108th?”

“Your first companies are moving toward Besh.”

Kota looked back through the side door.

The fires of Oros were much closer now.

“Good.”

Turnmaash paused.

“You’re taking the regiment into Besh yourself.”

It wasn’t a question.

Kota’s answer was immediate.

“That’s the plan.”

Turnmaash gave a quiet breath that might have been a laugh.

“Figured as much.”

Kota looked down at the city.

“Kel-Zor can keep them occupied. We’ll hit them head-on.”

“That should do it.”

No argument.

No warning.

Turnmaash trusted him.

Kota lowered his wrist comlink.

“Keep the other fronts supplied.”

“I will.”

The transmission ended.

Kota turned back toward the side door.

Oros filled more of the horizon now.

The once-brilliant capital of Kalyph was burning beneath a sky filled with smoke.

Rainer’s voice came through the internal comm again.

“General.”

“Yes?”

“You’re going to want to hold on, General.”

Kota tightened his grip on the overhead strap.

“Why?”

Rainer’s voice remained perfectly calm.

“We’re going in low, General.”

Kota looked out at the battlefield.

“Then fly.”

The LAAT dropped toward Oros.

## CHP2 Scene 4 – Arrival

The first thing the soldiers heard was the screaming.

Not people.

Engines.

LAAT gunships tore through the smoke-filled sky above Oros, their engines roaring as they descended toward the city.

Below them, the battle had already begun.

The capital was burning.

The once-brilliant skyline of Oros had been transformed.

The city’s enormous towers still rose into the morning sky, their glass viewports reflecting the first light of dawn. Neon advertisements continued flickering across some of the surviving buildings, casting patches of color through the smoke.

But the beauty of the city had been swallowed by war.

Entire sections of the skyline were blackened.

Several buildings burned from their upper floors.

Smoke poured from streets.

Speeders raced between towers.

Blaster fire flashed through intersections.

The massive planetary deflector shield that had once protected Oros from Kalyph’s violent electromagnetic storms and iron dust was gone.

The Separatists had destroyed it.

Without it, the city was exposed.

And they had come anyway.

The LAATs descended.

A gunship passed between two skyscrapers, barely clearing the buildings.

Another followed.

Then another.

Their formation broke apart as they approached the landing zone.

Below them, LZ-Besh was barely recognizable as a landing zone.

It had been established in the middle of a broad avenue outside the main fighting.

Now it was packed with soldiers.

Militia troops rushed between transports.

Medics dragged wounded soldiers away from the landing area.

Ammunition crates were being unloaded.

AT-TEs moved through the smoke.

Republic vehicles raced toward the city.

The ground shook.

A Separatist artillery shell struck several blocks away.

The explosion rose above the buildings.

The LAATs didn’t slow.

They came in low.

“Incoming!”

Ground crews scattered.

The first gunship descended.

Its repulsors kicked up dust and debris.

The sliding blast doors threw open before the gunship had completely settled.

Militia soldiers jumped from the troop bay.

“Move! Move!”

Another LAAT came down beside it.

Then another.

Then five more.

The 108th Regiment had arrived.

Clone troopers poured from the gunships.

Cresh Company immediately snapped to attention.

Ruptura stiffened.

“General Kel-Zor.”

Kel-Zor walked closer.

Ruptura straightened.

“Sir, I was simply directing assets to preserve human life.”

Kel-Zor stopped in front of him.

“Silence.”

Ruptura’s jaw tightened.

Kel-Zor’s cybernetic eye flickered.

“You’re letting your personal bitterness hijack a critical military operation.”

He looked toward Trench Squad.

“I don’t care where a soldier was born, Ruptura.”

His gaze moved across the clones and militia alike.

“Anyone wearing General Kota’s crest and bleeding in these streets is a soldier of the Line.”

Kel-Zor pointed toward the theater avenue.

“They are equals.”

Ruptura said nothing.

“You just tried to throw three of Kota’s elite soldiers into a fortified position because they were clones.”

Ruptura’s face reddened.

“That isn’t what I—”

“You’re relieved of command.”

Ruptura froze.

Kel-Zor continued.

“Effective immediately.”

The captain stared at him.

Kel-Zor turned toward two soldiers from his heavy vanguard.

“Lieutenants.”

The two soldiers stepped forward.

“Disarm Captain Ruptura.”

Ruptura’s hand tightened around his blaster.

“General—”

“Now.”

The lieutenants removed the weapon from his belt.

Ruptura resisted.

“My men need me!”

The soldiers seized his arms.

“You’re breaking the spine of this brigade!”

Kel-Zor didn’t move.

“For what?”

Ruptura struggled against the guards.

“For them?”

He pointed toward the clones.

“They aren’t even people!”

Ash went still.

Ruptura’s voice cracked.

“They’re property!”

Kel-Zor’s expression hardened.

“You’re finished.”

Ruptura continued shouting as the soldiers dragged him backward.

“You can’t take their side over real men!”

The soldiers pulled him toward the smoke.

“Kota is blind!”

Kel-Zor watched him.

“And you’re letting these things hollow us out from the inside!”

Ruptura’s voice echoed through the buildings.

“You’re building a house on sand, Kel-Zor!”

He fought against the guards again.

“You’ll see!”

His voice began to fade into the distance.

“You’ll all see what they really are!”

Then the gunfire swallowed him.

Silence settled over Cresh Company.

Not peace.

Just silence.

Kel-Zor looked at the soldiers.

“Get back to your positions.”

The company slowly moved.

Ash looked toward the wounded members of Trench Squad.

Kel-Zor turned toward him.

“Sergeant Ash.”

Ash straightened.

“General.”

“Private Novek.”

Novek stepped forward.

“Sir.”

Kel-Zor looked at the casualties.

“My vanguard will take care of them.”

Two medics immediately moved forward.

“We’ll get your wounded back to the medical extraction zone.”

Ash nodded.

“Thank you, General.”

Novek looked toward the wounded clone.

“What about him?”

“He comes with us.”

Kel-Zor gestured toward the clone.

“My men will make sure he gets there alive.”

The clone nodded.

“Thank you, General.”

Kel-Zor turned toward the theater avenue.

Then he looked back at Ash.

“You still have a mission.”

Ash nodded.

“Yes, General.”

“The senator?”

“Still trapped in the capital.”

Kel-Zor looked toward the avenue.

“Then we’re going after him.”

Ash hesitated.

“General, with respect, Trench Squad is down to three.”

Kel-Zor shook his head.

“Not anymore.”

He turned toward his heavy vanguard.

“Vanguard!”

The soldiers stepped forward.

Kel-Zor pointed toward the avenue.

“You’re going with Trench Squad.”

The heavy infantry immediately checked their weapons.

Ash looked at the soldiers.

Then at Kel-Zor.

“You’re reinforcing us?”

“I’m leading the reinforcement.”

Ash’s eyes widened slightly.

Kel-Zor looked toward the city.

“Cresh Company will continue clearing the residential sector.”

He pointed toward the avenue.

“My vanguard will handle the bunker.”

Then he looked at Ash.

“You handle the senator.”

Ash nodded.

“Understood.”

Kel-Zor looked at the remaining Cresh soldiers.

“Anyone who wants to come with us, fall in.”

Several veterans immediately stepped forward.

Then several more.

The company began reorganizing around the new objective.

The three remaining members of Trench Squad suddenly found themselves surrounded by dozens of soldiers.

Ash checked his weapon.

Novek stepped beside him.

“You know,” Novek said quietly, “I think we’re getting a bigger squad.”

Ash looked at the growing formation.

“Looks that way.”

Rogue Zero ignited his blue lightsaber.

The hum filled the intersection.

Kel-Zor drew his weapon.

“Sergeant.”

Ash looked at him.

Kel-Zor pointed toward the theater district.

“Get your squad moving.”

Ash nodded.

“Trench Squad.”

The three remaining clones raised their rifles.

Novek checked his weapon.

Ash looked toward Rogue Zero.

Then Kel-Zor.

“Let’s go.”

The massive strike force moved.

Rogue Zero led the way, his blue lightsaber illuminating the smoke.

Kel-Zor followed beside him, heavy mechanical footsteps striking the permacrete with every step.

Behind them came Trench Squad and the reinforced soldiers of the Republic.

The Separatists had expected a broken squad to crawl toward the capital.

Instead, the battle had given them something else.

A force.

And it was coming straight down the theater district avenue.

  

## CHP2 Scene 8 — The Capital Building

The fighting had followed them almost all the way to the capital.

Trench Squad moved through the last avenue with Kel-Zor’s vanguard surrounding them. Blasterfire still flashed between the buildings behind them, but the sound was beginning to fade as the combined force pushed deeper into the capital district.

The capital building finally came into view.

It rose above the surrounding structures like a monument of white durasteel and transparisteel, its upper towers disappearing into the smoke-choked sky. Several sections of the facade had been scorched, and one of the upper windows had been shattered completely.

But the building was still standing.

More importantly, its perimeter had not been breached.

Kalyph Defense Force barricades formed a defensive ring around the entire complex. Heavy portable barriers had been arranged behind the main gate, reinforced with sandbag emplacements, portable shield generators, and anti-infantry positions.

And scattered across the ground outside the barricades were dozens upon dozens of destroyed battle droids.

Some had been reduced to piles of twisted metal.

Others lay in pieces around the barricades, their blasters still clutched in lifeless hands.

Kel-Zor slowed.

He looked across the perimeter.

Then at the gate.

Then back at the wreckage.

His cybernetic eye narrowed.

They had fought their way through entire streets filled with Separatist resistance. They had seen fortified positions, armored units, and countless battle droids.

Yet here, the capital’s defensive line looked almost untouched.

Kel-Zor stood silently for several seconds.

Ash stopped beside him.

“General?”

Kel-Zor raised one hand.

The squad halted.

Nobody spoke.

Then the main gate began to open.

The soldiers immediately raised their weapons.

KDF troops appeared behind the barricades, rifles trained outward.

But they weren’t battle droids.

They were human soldiers.

Their armor was battered and blackened from combat, but they were standing.

A Kalyph Defense Force colonel stepped through the gate, followed by several soldiers.

He looked at Kel-Zor, then at the troops behind him.

For the first time in what seemed like hours, the man’s expression softened.

“Friendly soldiers,” he said with a tired laugh. “I’d almost forgotten what those looked like.”

Kel-Zor lowered his weapon.

“So had we.”

The colonel gave a weary smile.

“You’re with General Kota?”

“Yes.”

“Then you’re welcome.”

The colonel stepped aside.

Kel-Zor looked past him toward the capital building.

“We’re here for Senator Corin Trotz.”

“He’s inside.”

“Then take me to him.”

The colonel nodded.

“Follow me.”

The gate opened wider.

Kel-Zor’s troops moved through first, followed by Trench Squad. As they entered the defensive perimeter, Ash looked around at the KDF positions.

There were far more soldiers here than he had expected.

Heavy weapons had been positioned at every major approach. Ammunition crates were stacked behind the barricades. Several wounded soldiers were being treated by medics while others redistributed power cells and checked their rifles.

This wasn’t simply a guard detail.

It was a fortress within the fortress.

Kel-Zor noticed it too.

“How long have you been holding this position?”

The colonel kept walking.

“Since the first assault reached the government district.”

“And you haven’t lost the perimeter?”

“Not once.”

Kel-Zor glanced toward the wreckage outside.

“Impressive.”

The colonel gave a short laugh.

“You should see what happens when people threaten the senator’s doorstep.”

They entered the capital building.

The doors sealed behind them, cutting off much of the noise outside.

Inside, the grand hallway had been transformed into another defensive position. KDF soldiers occupied balconies and intersections. Portable barricades blocked several corridors. Officers moved between positions carrying ammunition and reports.

The colonel led Kel-Zor through the center of it all.

Trench Squad and the rest of Kel-Zor’s troops followed until they reached a large set of doors.

The colonel stopped.

“Senator’s secure office.”

Kel-Zor looked at his troops.

“You stay here.”

Ash nodded.

Kel-Zor pointed toward the corridor.

“Nobody gets through this door.”

“Yes, General.”

The colonel motioned for Kel-Zor to follow him.

The two walked toward the doors.

Behind them, Trench Squad took up defensive positions.

Kel-Zor stopped beside the colonel.

The colonel pressed his hand against the control panel.

The doors slid apart.

Inside, the room was surprisingly quiet.

The Senator stood beside a shattered window.

Beyond the broken transparisteel, Oros burned.

Smoke drifted between the towers. Fires consumed entire sections of the city. Blasterfire streaked through the distant streets, bright even beneath the sunlit sky.

Senator Corin Trotz stood with his hands clasped behind his back.

His robes were immaculate except for a smear of ash across one sleeve.

He turned.

A slight, weary smile appeared on his face.

“Ah, General.”

Kel-Zor entered.

The colonel remained near the doorway.

The Senator looked past him.

“You’re here.”

“We came as quickly as we could.”

“I would have preferred slightly quicker.”

The Senator chuckled softly.

Kel-Zor stared at him.

“We had to fight through half the Separatist army.”

“Oh, I noticed.”

The Senator gestured toward the shattered window.

“I could hear most of it over our own gunfire.”

He looked back toward Kel-Zor.

“The Separatists must have committed a massive portion of their forces just to hold you back for as long as they did.”

Kel-Zor glanced around the room.

“Well, we’re here now.”

His eyes moved toward the hallway beyond the office.

“And I see you and your guard have dealt with the droids yourselves.”

The Senator smiled.

“Of course.”

He gestured toward the building.

“I keep the best of the best around me. Why wouldn’t I?”

He walked toward Kel-Zor.

“I would be a fool not to keep a planetary response unit stationed outside my door.”

Kel-Zor’s expression remained unchanged.

The Senator stopped a few feet away.

“But…”

His eyes narrowed slightly.

“I must admit, General, I’m surprised.”

“About what?”

“You managed to punch through that blockade with the numbers you officially have.”

Kel-Zor said nothing.

The Senator smiled.

“It’s quite impressive.”

He swirled his fingers together, looking thoughtfully down at his hands.

“I didn’t know Kota’s forces had reached the level of Republic Clone Commandos.”

Kel-Zor almost smiled.

“We have some of the best soldiers in the militia.”

“Apparently.”

The Senator looked toward him.

“But I wouldn’t say we’re quite at the level of Clone Commandos.”

“Still,” Kel-Zor said, “I’ll take the compliment on behalf of the men.”

The Senator went entirely still.

For a moment, the humor disappeared from his face.

“General…”

Kel-Zor looked at him.

“You need to learn to drop the act sometimes.”

Kel-Zor stiffened slightly.

“Senator?”

“Did you really think you could fool me today?”

The Senator slowly looked around the room.

“Look around us.”

He gestured toward the burning city outside.

“I knew a long time ago that the troop numbers on your official Republic manifests were never right.”

Kel-Zor remained silent.

The Senator continued.

“I’m in the Senate, General. I know how the bureaucracy tracks assets.”

He walked toward his desk and leaned against it, crossing his arms.

“Tell me something.”

Kel-Zor waited.

“Does Kota lie to the Jedi Council as well?”

That got a reaction.

Only a slight one.

But the Senator noticed it.

“I can’t imagine those rigid monks would ever approve of a volunteer militia anywhere near the size of the one you actually have.”

The Senator shook his head with an amused smile.

“Don’t worry.”

He raised a hand.

“I’m not going to rat out my protectors.”

Kel-Zor studied him carefully.

The room was silent for several seconds.

Then he exhaled.

“To be honest, Senator…”

He looked toward the burning city.

“I thought you would have figured it out a long time ago.”

The Senator laughed quietly.

“Why?”

“Because of the logistical support we’ve been able to provide across this entire sector.”

Kel-Zor folded his arms.

“It’s hard to hide that much food and ammunition.”

The Senator shook his head.

“You’d be surprised.”

He pushed himself away from the desk.

“There are a lot of things you have to do as a Senator.”

He walked slowly toward the window.

“Sometimes you don’t have the luxury of paying attention to every discrepancy in a shipping manifest.”

He looked down at the burning streets.

“But the thought crossed my mind more than once.”

Kel-Zor waited.

“How were you able to accomplish everything you did with the meager numbers you officially reported?”

The Senator looked back at him.

“I suppose I have my answer now.”

Kel-Zor didn’t deny it.

“Lie to the Senate.”

The Senator’s expression became more serious.

“Build your numbers in the shadows.”

Kel-Zor nodded.

“Build what you actually need to survive.”

The Senator stared at him for a moment.

Then he gave a quiet, almost approving laugh.

“Quite a strategy.”

“It’s the only one that kept us alive.”

The Senator’s expression hardened.

“I imagine Coruscant wouldn’t have been particularly understanding.”

“No.”

Kel-Zor’s voice dropped.

“If we had been honest about our recruitment numbers, it would have caused a political crisis in the Republic.”

He stepped closer.

“Coruscant would have panicked. The Senate would have demanded that Kota disband the majority of our forces or reduce them to the tiny numbers listed on paper.”

“And then?”

“We would have been left defenseless.”

The Senator looked out across the burning city.

“And today…”

His voice became quieter.

“This capital would have fallen.”

Kel-Zor said nothing.

“I would have been taken prisoner.”

The Senator paused.

“Or worse.”

Silence filled the office.

Then the Senator extended his hand.

“So consider this off the record, General.”

Kel-Zor looked at the offered hand.

“I’m glad you lied.”

He shook Kel-Zor’s hand firmly.

“Tell General Kota that his lies saved a world today.”

The Senator released his hand.

“And he has my silence for as long as this war lasts.”

Kel-Zor nodded.

“I’ll pass along the message, Senator.”

He turned toward the door.

“There’s one more problem.”

The Senator raised an eyebrow.

“What?”

Kel-Zor looked toward the shattered window.

“This city isn’t secure.”

The Senator’s expression sobered.

“How bad?”

“Bad enough that Kota sent us here to extract you.”

Kel-Zor gestured toward the hallway.

“Your forces held this building, but the Separatists are still fighting across the capital.”

“I have a planetary response unit here.”

“And you’ve already lost soldiers.”

The Senator didn’t answer.

Kel-Zor continued.

“We’re moving you somewhere safer.”

The Senator glanced toward the burning skyline.

For the first time, the weight of the battle seemed to settle over him.

“Where?”

“Somewhere the Separatists aren’t expecting.”

The Senator considered that.

Then nodded.

“Very well.”

He straightened his robes and looked toward the doorway.

“Let’s go.”

The door opened.

The KDF colonel immediately stepped forward.

“Senator?”

“We’re leaving.”

The colonel blinked.

“Sir?”

“General Kel-Zor has orders to extract me.”

The colonel looked toward Kel-Zor.

Kel-Zor nodded.

“His orders are to get you out of the city.”

The colonel didn’t argue.

He immediately turned toward the hallway.

“You heard him! Begin evacuation protocol.”

KDF soldiers moved.

Barricades were dismantled. Ammunition crates were sealed. Medical teams gathered their equipment. Soldiers began pulling defensive positions apart and preparing transports.

Trench Squad watched as the defenders who had held the capital building through the brutal siege transformed from a stationary garrison into an evacuation force.

Ash looked toward Novek.

Novek simply shook his head.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Novek glanced at the burning city outside.

“Just didn’t expect rescuing a politician to be this much of a bloodbath.”

Ash looked at the soldiers moving around them.

“Everyone gets rescued.”

Novek smiled faintly.

“Yeah.”

Kel-Zor stepped into the hallway.

“Move.”

Trench Squad immediately fell into formation.

Behind them, Senator Corin Trotz followed with the KDF colonel and his personal guard.

Outside, the capital was still burning.

But the senator was alive.

And for the first time since the battle began, the mission Kota had given them was complete.

  

## CHP2 Scene 9 — Quiet After the Storm

Two hours after the Senator’s evacuation, Oros was finally secure.

The fighting had stopped.

The Separatist assault had been broken, the surviving battle droids had either been destroyed or driven from the capital district, and Kota’s forces had begun establishing a defensive perimeter around the city.

But Oros was far from peaceful.

Smoke drifted between the towers.

Fires still burned in several districts.

Destroyed speeders and battle droids littered the streets, while Kalyph Defense Force soldiers and Republic troops moved through the ruins searching for survivors and securing damaged buildings.

The city was exhausted.

So were its defenders.

Rogue Zero walked alone through the streets.

His blue lightsaber hung from his belt. His armor was scorched from the fighting, and dust covered the lower half of his robes.

He had been searching for Kota.

Instead, he found someone else.

A group of clone troopers were gathered around a damaged vehicle at an intersection. One of them stood apart from the others, studying a tactical display.

Rogue stopped.

The clone turned.

For a moment, neither said anything.

Then the clone removed his helmet.

Commander Vantage stared at him.

A faint smile appeared.

“Well.”

Rogue smiled back.

“Commander.”

Vantage looked him over.

“Been a long time.”

“Too long.”

Vantage stepped closer.

“I heard you were dead.”

“I’ve heard that one before.”

“Apparently you have a talent for disappointing people.”

Rogue gave a quiet laugh.

“Good to see you too.”

Vantage looked at the lightsaber hanging from his belt.

“Still carrying that.”

“Still carrying yours?”

Vantage glanced at his blaster.

“Something like it.”

The two stood there for a moment.

There was an ease between them that didn’t come from a recent battlefield alliance.

They had known each other long before Oros.

Back when the Clone Wars were still new.

Before either of them had any idea how long the fighting would continue.

Before the Republic had spent years bleeding across the galaxy.

Vantage looked toward the ruined avenue.

“You remember Malgoiim?”

Rogue smiled.

“Unfortunately.”

“You remember the bridge?”

“I remember you telling me not to jump.”

“I specifically told you not to jump.”

“And I survived.”

“You landed on a tank.”

“It broke my fall.”

Vantage shook his head.

“You were impossible.”

“You weren’t exactly pleasant company.”

“I was a clone commander.”

“Exactly.”

Vantage laughed.

The sound was brief, but genuine.

For a few seconds, neither looked like soldiers standing in the aftermath of a battle.

They looked like old comrades who had unexpectedly found each other again.

Vantage eventually put his helmet beneath his arm.

“I never did figure out where you disappeared to.”

Rogue’s expression became more guarded.

“Had places to be.”

“That’s what I figured.”

Vantage didn’t press.

He knew better.

Instead, he looked toward the avenue.

“So.”

Rogue glanced at him.

“What?”

“What brings you back?”

Rogue looked toward the distant command positions.

“I was looking for Kota.”

Vantage raised an eyebrow.

“Of course you were.”

“Do you know where he is?”

Vantage turned slightly.

“He should be—”

“Looking for me?”

Kota’s voice came from behind them.

Rogue turned.

Kota walked toward them through the smoke.

His armor was blackened and scratched from the fighting. His green lightsaber hung at his side.

Vantage looked between the two Jedi.

Then he gave a small nod.

“I’ll leave you two alone.”

Kota looked at him.

“Thanks.”

Vantage replaced his helmet.

He started walking away, then stopped.

“Good seeing you again.”

Rogue nodded.

“You too.”

Vantage continued down the street, disappearing among the clones.

Rogue turned back toward Kota.

For a moment, the two simply looked at each other.

There was no surprise in Kota’s expression.

No confusion.

No question about who Rogue Zero was.

He already knew.

Rogue slowly approached him.

“Master.”

Kota’s eyes remained fixed on him.

“Ronen.”

The name was quiet.

Almost lost beneath the distant sounds of the city.

But it was enough.

The young Jedi’s expression softened.

Kota looked him over.

“You look terrible.”

Ronen glanced down at his scorched armor.

“Good to see you too, Master.”

Kota’s mouth twitched.

“How long have you been on Kalyph?”

“Not long.”

“I know.”

Ronen looked toward the ruined capital.

“You’ve been busy.”

“So have you.”

Kota’s eyes briefly moved toward the blue lightsaber.

“You found Trench Squad.”

“I did.”

“How are they?”

Ronen’s expression became mischievous.

“Dead.”

Kota stopped.

His expression hardened immediately.

“What?”

Ronen held the expression for another second.

Then smiled.

“Joking.”

Kota stared at him.

“They’re alive.”

“All of them?”

“Wounded. None killed.”

Kota exhaled.

“Don’t do that again.”

Ronen smiled.

“Couldn’t resist.”

Kota shook his head.

“Still reckless.”

“Still your Padawan.”

Kota looked at him for a moment.

Then his expression softened.

“Apparently.”

They began walking together.

For a few moments, neither spoke.

Then Ronen’s expression became serious.

“Master.”

Kota looked toward him.

“I received information from one of the Network contacts.”

Kota’s expression changed immediately.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

The Network.

For nearly two years, Ronen had been moving through worlds and trade routes far beyond the Jedi Order’s normal reach. He had built contacts, secured supply routes, and strengthened relationships that the Republic could never officially acknowledge.

As far as the Jedi Order and the Republic knew, Ronen Rozdelyt had disappeared.

Missing in action.

That was the story Kota had deliberately given them.

Only a handful of people knew otherwise.

Kota lowered his voice.

“What kind of information?”

“Something important.”

“How important?”

“Important enough that I came here.”

Kota studied him.

Ronen continued.

“I don’t think we should discuss it in the open.”

Kota glanced around.

Clones and KDF soldiers moved through the surrounding streets. Officers were still carrying reports between command posts. Communications traffic filled the air.

He nodded.

“We won’t.”

Ronen looked at him.

“When?”

“Once we’re somewhere secure.”

Ronen nodded.

Kota continued walking.

“Until then, you’re still Rogue Zero.”

Ronen looked at him.

“Understood.”

Kota glanced back.

“And as far as anyone in this army is concerned…”

Ronen finished the thought.

“Ronen Rozdelyt is still missing.”

Kota gave a faint nod.

“Exactly.”

The two Jedi continued down the ruined avenue together.

Behind them, the soldiers of the Republic and Kalyph Defense Force continued securing Oros.

Ahead of them waited a conversation that could not be trusted to open air, military channels, or even the walls of the capital.

For now, the war had gone quiet.

But whatever Ronen had learned from the Network was about to change that.
